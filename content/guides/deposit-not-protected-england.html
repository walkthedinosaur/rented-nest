Last reviewed 12 November 2026 · Covers private renters in England only. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: check all three deposit schemes first – it takes a couple of minutes. If your deposit isn't in any of them, your landlord should have protected it within 30 days of you paying it and given you written information about the scheme. Shelter says that if they didn't, you may be able to get your deposit back plus compensation of up to three times the deposit – often by settling in writing, sometimes through a court claim.

Not in England? Different rules apply – see private residential tenancies in Scotland, Renting Homes in Wales and private renting in Northern Ireland.

How to check if your deposit is protected

There are three government-approved schemes, and each has an online deposit checker. Shelter's check your deposit page (updated 1 May 2026, checked 12 November 2026) says you'll need:

  • your postcode
  • your surname
  • your tenancy start date
  • the deposit amount

Search all three: the Deposit Protection Service, mydeposits and the Tenancy Deposit Scheme. If nothing comes up, Shelter suggests trying a joint tenant's surname (or the lead tenant's, if you have one), and asking your landlord which scheme they used and what name they registered it under.

Who this applies to: Shelter says the rules cover assured and assured shorthold tenancies – most private renters. Lodgers' deposits don't have to be protected.

It was called an "advance payment" or "holding fee" – is it still a deposit?

What matters is what the money is for, not what it's called. Shelter's legal guidance on tenancy deposit rules defines a deposit as money held as security for the tenant's obligations – in plain words, money the landlord keeps in case of damage or unpaid rent and is supposed to give back at the end.

  • Rent in advance is not a deposit. If the money was simply your first month's rent, paid early and used as rent, it doesn't need protecting.
  • A holding deposit is different too. That's money paid to hold a property before you sign. Shelter says it can't be more than one week's rent, and GOV.UK says it becomes a deposit that must be protected once you become a tenant.
  • Money you expect back when you leave, whatever the receipt calls it, is likely to be a deposit. If you're not sure, ask Shelter or Citizens Advice to look at your paperwork.

What you can do: ask, settle, or claim

From Shelter's pages on an unprotected deposit (updated 1 May 2026) and making a compensation claim (updated 20 July 2026), both checked 12 November 2026:

  1. Ask in writing. Ask your landlord to confirm which scheme your deposit is in (letter below). Sometimes it's simply registered under a different name.
  2. Get advice. Talk to Shelter or Citizens Advice before you make demands – especially if you still live there.
  3. Settle in writing. Shelter says most landlords don't want to go to court and may agree to pay compensation if you agree not to. Any settlement offer should be in writing. If you owe rent, Shelter says you could ask the landlord to write off the arrears instead.
  4. Send a letter before action. Before going to court you must send a formal letter to the landlord and any letting agent setting out your claim. Shelter has a template, and says landlords sometimes settle at this point.
  5. Claim in the county court. The court could order the landlord to pay 1 to 3 times the deposit. Shelter says you use a Part 8 claim form (N208), the fee to start is £387 (you get it back if you win, not if you lose), and you may get help with court fees on a low income.

We haven't found a primary source stating the time limit for a compensation claim, so don't assume you have years – get advice soon. And no outcome is guaranteed: the court decides the amount.

Copy-and-adapt letter: "Please confirm where my deposit is protected"

This is a first, polite step – it doesn't threaten anything. Keep a copy and note the date you sent it.

Subject: Tenancy deposit – [property address]

Dear [landlord or agent's name],

I paid a deposit of £[amount] on [date] for my tenancy at [address], which started on [date].

I've searched the Deposit Protection Service, mydeposits and the Tenancy Deposit Scheme and can't find it. Please could you tell me, within 14 days:

1. which scheme my deposit is protected with, and the deposit ID or certificate number;
2. the date it was protected and the name it is registered under; and
3. a copy of the written information about the deposit protection that you gave me.

Kind regards,
[Your name]
[Date]

If the reply confirms it was never protected, or protected late, that's the point to get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice about the next step.

Does an unprotected deposit stop an eviction?

Partly. Shelter says that since 1 May 2026 private landlords can't give section 21 "no fault" notices at all. Under section 8, in some cases the court can't order an eviction if the deposit wasn't protected or the written information wasn't given – but other grounds may still allow it. If you've had a notice, read got a section 8 notice? What to do and get advice straight away.

A deposit problem doesn't make it safe to stop paying rent. Rent arrears are a separate ground for eviction.

An old deposit you never got back

If a tenancy ended a while ago and you never got your deposit back, it's still worth chasing:

  • Find out which scheme holds it. The written information from the start of your tenancy, or the deposit ID, should say. It may be a different scheme from the one you're looking in.
  • Contact the scheme directly – by phone or its contact form, not just the online account – with the deposit ID, the address, the tenancy dates and your name as registered. Shelter says the 0300 and 0333 numbers aren't free but are usually included in call bundles.
  • Ask the landlord or agent in writing to confirm the scheme and deposit ID, and whether the deposit has been paid out to anyone.
  • If it wasn't protected at all, follow the steps above and get advice.

If your landlord is now trying to take money off a protected deposit, see how to dispute deposit deductions.

FAQ

How long does my landlord have to protect my deposit?

30 days from when you paid it, and they must give you written information about the scheme within the same 30 days.

How much compensation can I get?

Shelter says a court could order 1 to 3 times the deposit – up to £3,000 on a £1,000 deposit – as well as the deposit itself. The court decides the amount; nothing is automatic.

Can I claim after I've moved out?

Shelter's guidance says the rules also cover assured shorthold tenancies that ended before 1 May 2026. It doesn't give a time limit for claiming, and we haven't verified one – so get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice as soon as you can.

Do lodgers' deposits have to be protected?

No. If you live with your landlord as a lodger, the deposit doesn't need to be in a scheme.

Will my landlord evict me if I raise this?

Section 21 "no fault" notices ended on 1 May 2026, and an unprotected deposit can stop some section 8 evictions. If you're worried about how your landlord will react, talk to Shelter or Citizens Advice before you write.
