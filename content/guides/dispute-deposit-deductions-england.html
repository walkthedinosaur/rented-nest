Last reviewed 12 November 2026 · Covers private renters in England only. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: don't agree to the deductions yet. Your deposit is your money, and Shelter says any deduction has to be reasonable and relate to money your landlord has actually lost. They can't charge for fair wear and tear, can't keep money just because the place is due a redecoration, and can't make you pay for a professional clean. If you can't agree, the deposit stays protected and the scheme's dispute service is free.

Still packing up? Our move-out deposit checklist covers cleaning and evidence before you leave. This page is for after your landlord or agent has said they want to keep some of it.

Not in England? Different rules apply – see private residential tenancies in Scotland, Renting Homes in Wales and private renting in Northern Ireland.

What a landlord can and can't charge for

The starting point is your check-in inventory: you only have to hand the home back in the same state it was in when you moved in, allowing for normal use. You don't have to improve it. From Shelter's pages on cleaning and decorating and damage and missing items (both checked 12 November 2026):

Usually can't be chargedCan be charged (a reasonable amount)
A professional clean because the contract says so – Shelter says a clause making you pay for one is unfair and can't be enforcedCleaning to get the home back to the standard on the check-in report, if you left it dirtier
Redecorating just because it's dueRedecorating after you painted walls a different colour or made other changes without consent
Fair wear and tear: small scuffs, faded curtains, worn or frayed carpetDamage beyond normal use – an uncleanable stain or a broken item
Damp and mould that's the landlord's responsibility, or damage from repairs they were slow to doCondensation damage, but only if you caused it
The full cost of a brand-new replacement for an old itemA share of the cost, based on the item's age and quality

Shelter defines fair wear and tear as changes caused by normal everyday living, and says a family leaving after five years is allowed more of it than one person leaving after six months. A "professional standard" clean can only be asked for if the home was professionally cleaned before you moved in – and even then you can do it yourself to that standard.

If the claim is about mould, check whose job it was first: our guide to black mould in a rented flat explains when it's down to your landlord.

Why the age of the carpet or paintwork matters

Carpets, paint and furniture wear out on their own. So even when you did cause damage, your landlord generally can't make you pay for a brand-new replacement. Shelter's own example:

  • A stain that can be cleaned: the landlord should only keep the cost of cleaning it (Shelter uses £50 against a £250 carpet).
  • A stain that can't be cleaned, on a 5-year-old carpet: if a similar new carpet costs £250, taking about £125 might be reasonable.
  • If the landlord upgrades: buying a £500 carpet instead doesn't change that – Shelter says they should still only keep about £125.

Shelter says the full replacement cost is usually only fair if you hadn't lived there long or the item was new when you moved in. So the questions to ask are: how old was it, what was it like at check-in, and is the price for a like-for-like replacement? The same thinking applies to a "full redecoration" after several years – paintwork that's simply tired is wear and tear.

Step by step: replying to a deduction

  1. Don't agree on the phone. Ask for everything in writing. Once you've agreed an amount, GOV.UK says the landlord has 10 days to return the rest – so be sure before you say yes.
  2. Ask for an itemised list. Each deduction, how much, and the evidence: quotes or invoices, check-out photos, and the age of anything they want to replace.
  3. Find your evidence. The check-in inventory, your own move-in and move-out photos and videos, cleaning receipts, and any emails where you reported repairs or got permission for changes.
  4. Check prices. Shelter suggests checking replacement costs or getting your own quote if you think you're being overcharged.
  5. Say what you accept. You can agree to a small amount for one genuine item and dispute the rest. Shelter says you can dispute an amount even if you accept you caused the damage.
  6. Reply in writing, using the email below, and keep copies of everything.

Copy-and-adapt email: "I don't agree to these deductions"

Delete the lines that don't apply. Stick to facts – the person who decides a dispute will read this.

Subject: Deposit deductions – [property address] – deposit ID [number, if you have it]

Dear [landlord or agent's name],

Thank you for your email of [date] proposing to deduct £[amount] from my deposit. I don't agree to these deductions.

Cleaning: I cleaned the property to the standard recorded in the check-in inventory dated [date]. My photos from [move-out date] are attached. I am not required to pay for a professional clean, and the home was not professionally cleaned before I moved in. [Delete this last sentence if it was.]

Carpets / decorating: I lived at the property for [number] years. The [carpet / paintwork] was [age, if known, or "not new"] when I moved in, and the wear shown is fair wear and tear. Please send the age of the [carpet / decoration], the date it was last replaced, and quotes or invoices for the work.

[I accept £[amount] for [item], and ask you to return the remaining £[amount].]

Please send an itemised list of any deductions you still propose, with your evidence, within 14 days. If we can't agree, I will ask [scheme name] to resolve the dispute.

Kind regards,
[Your name]
[Forwarding address]
[Date]

Using the free deposit dispute service

If your deposit is protected, it sits with one of three government-approved schemes: the Deposit Protection Service (DPS), mydeposits or the Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS). GOV.UK's disputes page (checked 12 November 2026) says:

  • Each scheme has a free dispute resolution service.
  • It's optional – you and your landlord both have to agree to use it.
  • You'll both be asked for evidence, and the decision is final.
  • While you disagree, the deposit stays protected in the scheme.

Time limits vary by scheme – don't wait. For example, mydeposits says (checked 12 November 2026) that for an insured deposit you must act within three calendar months of moving out, and for a custodial deposit within 90 days of the release being started. We haven't verified the current limits for DPS or TDS – check your scheme's website, or the written information you were given when your deposit was protected, as soon as a deduction is proposed.

What evidence wins. The adjudicator only sees paperwork, so a signed check-in inventory, dated photos, receipts and your emails do the talking. Shelter says a scheme looking at wear and tear will consider who lived in the home and for how long.

Not sure? Shelter and Citizens Advice can look at your case for free.

If your deposit was never protected

If you can't find your deposit in any of the three schemes, there's no dispute service to use – but your landlord may have broken the law. Landlords must protect most deposits within 30 days. Shelter says you may be able to claim the deposit back plus compensation of up to three times its value. See our guide: my landlord didn't protect my deposit – what can I do?

FAQ

Can my landlord charge me for a professional clean?

Not just because the tenancy agreement says so. Shelter says a clause making you pay for professional cleaning is unfair and can't be enforced. You only have to clean to the standard on the check-in report – if you left it dirtier than that, the landlord can claim a reasonable cleaning cost.

They say the flat needs a full redecoration after 4 years. Do I have to pay?

Not for normal wear. Shelter says your landlord can't keep money just because it's time to redecorate. They can claim if you made changes without permission or caused damage that has to be put right before new tenants move in – and even then, the age of the paintwork counts.

What if there was no check-in inventory?

The landlord has to show the home was left in a worse state than when you moved in. Without an inventory that's much harder to prove, so gather whatever you have – move-in photos, emails, adverts showing the property – and say in your reply that there's no check-in record. Get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice if the amount is large.

Can I just not pay my last month's rent and let them keep the deposit?

Shelter says you should pay rent on the normal date unless you agree something else with your landlord, and to get any agreement in writing. If you simply don't pay, the landlord can keep the deposit to cover the rent – and you lose the chance to dispute other deductions from it.

How long does my landlord have to return my deposit?

GOV.UK says within 10 days of you both agreeing how much you'll get back. If you can't agree, the money stays protected until the dispute is sorted out.
