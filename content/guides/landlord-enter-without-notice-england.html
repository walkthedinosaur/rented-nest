Last reviewed 6 November 2026 · Covers private renters in England only. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: not normally. Shelter says your landlord or letting agent should tell you they're coming and ask if it's okay – and should not come in without your permission, even if they have a key. For an inspection they must give at least 24 hours' notice in writing and visit at a reasonable time of day. Coming in without notice or permission could be harassment.

It's still your home while you rent it. Below: what each kind of visit needs, when you can say no (and when saying no backfires), an email you can copy, and what to do if it keeps happening.

Inspections, repairs, safety checks and viewings

Different visits have different rules. This is what Shelter's guidance says for each:

VisitNoticeDo you have to agree?
InspectionAt least 24 hours, in writing, at a reasonable time of dayNot unless your tenancy agreement says you should – check it
Repairs you reportedReasonable notice – Shelter says this is usually at least 24 hoursYou should let them in so the repair can be done within a reasonable time
Emergency repairsCould be very short notice – but your landlord must tell you first and shouldn't just turn upTreat it like any reported repair – let them in so it can be fixed
Gas safety check (yearly) and electrical check (every 5 years)Agree a time; your landlord should confirm the date by email or letterShelter says to agree a time – refusing safety checks could put your safety at risk
Viewings (new tenants or buyers)No fixed period in Shelter's guidance; many agreements say "reasonable notice"Only if your tenancy agreement says you must – you can suggest a time

"A reasonable time of day" means not too early, not too late, and not when most people would be busy.

Can you say no?

Inspections and viewings: Shelter says you don't have to agree unless your tenancy agreement says you should. Read the agreement first – many do have a clause about access. Offering a different time is usually better than a flat no.

Repairs and safety checks: think carefully before refusing. Shelter warns that refusing access for repairs, inspections and safety checks could put your health and safety at risk and break the rules of your tenancy agreement, and your landlord might ask you to pay towards fixing a problem that got worse because you didn't let them in. Refusing viewings may also cause problems with references or your deposit.

If you want access but not on their terms, you can:

  • suggest another day or time in writing
  • ask a friend or neighbour you trust to be there
  • ask to see ID if you don't know the person at the door
  • agree that they can use their key while you're out, if you're comfortable with that

Waiting for a repair? See no heating or hot water and black mould in a rented flat for how to report it in writing first.

Copy-and-adapt emails

Keep it polite and factual – the point is a dated record. Change the words in [brackets].

1. Asking for notice before visits

Subject: Visits to [address]

Hi [name],

I'm happy to arrange access for inspections and repairs. Please could you give me at least 24 hours' notice in writing before any visit, with the date, a time and who will be coming, so I can make sure I'm available or arrange for someone to be here.

Please don't enter the property without agreeing a time with me first.

Thanks, [your name]

2. If it has already happened

Subject: Entry to [address] on [date]

Hi [name],

On [date] at about [time], [you / someone from your office] came into the flat without notice and without my agreement. I'd like to arrange visits in advance: please give me at least 24 hours' written notice and wait for me to confirm a time before entering, except in a genuine emergency.

I'm keeping a note of visits. I'd rather sort this out between us.

Thanks, [your name]

Save a copy of everything you send and receive. If you're thinking about moving on, see how to give notice to your landlord.

If your landlord keeps letting themselves in

Shelter lists "coming into your home without permission" as an example of harassment by a landlord or agent. Its suggested steps:

  • Keep a diary – dates and times of each visit, and what happened.
  • Keep evidence – texts, emails, letters, and the names of anyone who saw what happened.
  • Talk to other tenants in the building or house – dealing with it together can be easier.
  • Take your evidence to the council or the police. Many councils have a private housing or tenancy relations team that deals with harassment.

Get advice before things escalate: Shelter and Citizens Advice are free. Since 1 May 2026, landlords in England can no longer use section 21 "no-fault" evictions – see what the Renters' Rights Act changed.

House shares: your bedroom

If you rent just a room and share the kitchen and bathroom, Shelter says your landlord can come into the shared areas – but they still need a good reason, such as repairs, and must tell you when and why. They can't enter your bedroom unless you say it's okay, and they can't harass or threaten you in the shared areas either.

If a landlord who lives elsewhere keeps walking into the house unannounced, the same steps apply: put your request for notice in writing and keep a record.

FAQ

How much notice does a landlord have to give to enter in England?

For an inspection, at least 24 hours in writing, at a reasonable time of day. For repairs, reasonable notice – Shelter says usually at least 24 hours. Emergency repairs can be very short notice, but your landlord should still tell you first.

Can my landlord let himself in with a key when I'm out?

Not without your permission. Shelter says they should not enter without your permission, even if they have a key. You can agree to it for a particular visit if that suits you.

Can I refuse a landlord inspection?

You don't have to agree to inspections unless your tenancy agreement says you should. Refusing repairs or safety checks is different and can cause problems – suggest another time instead.

Is my landlord entering without permission harassment?

It could be. Shelter lists coming into your home without permission as an example of harassment. Keep a diary and evidence, and contact your council, Shelter or Citizens Advice.
