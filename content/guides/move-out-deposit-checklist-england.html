Last reviewed 29 October 2026 · Covers England. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: clean to the standard on your check-in inventory – not better – put back anything you changed, photograph every room on the day you leave, and ask for your deposit back in writing. Your landlord can't take money for fair wear and tear, or make you pay for professional cleaning. If you can't agree on deductions, your deposit scheme has a free dispute service.

Four weeks, one week, moving day

Not given notice yet? See how much notice you need to give your landlord (with a template).

About four weeks before

  • Find your check-in inventory and any photos you took when you moved in. This is the standard you'll be measured against.
  • Check your deposit is protected. In England it must be in one of three schemes – the Deposit Protection Service, mydeposits or the Tenancy Deposit Scheme – within 30 days of your landlord getting it (GOV.UK).
  • Make a list of anything you changed or added – hooks, shelves, a painted wall, a swapped light shade – and decide what has to come down.
  • Book a hire carpet cleaner or a cleaner only if you want to – Shelter says you don't have to use professional cleaning.

The week before

  • Deep-clean the rooms you can live without (spare room, cupboards, oven).
  • Book the bulky-waste collection if you have rubbish that won't fit in the bins.
  • Ask your landlord or agent when the check-out inspection is and whether you can be there.

Moving day

  • Final wipe-down once the furniture is out, then photograph and video every room.
  • Take meter readings with a dated photo, return every key and get a receipt for them.
  • Give the landlord your forwarding address and ask for the deposit back in writing (see the last section).

Room-by-room cleaning checklist

Shelter's rule of thumb: "You only need to clean your home to the same standard as it was when you moved in." Use your inventory as the checklist – if it says the oven was spotless, it needs to be spotless again.

  • Kitchen: oven, hob and grill pan; extractor hood and filter; inside the fridge and freezer (defrost it the day before); inside and on top of cupboards; tiles and grout behind the hob; sink, taps and plughole; under and behind appliances you can move.
  • Bathroom: limescale on taps, shower head, screen and tiles; toilet including under the rim and the base; extractor fan cover; any mould on sealant (see our black mould guide – report anything that keeps coming back rather than just covering it).
  • Every room: skirting boards, door frames and handles; light switches and sockets; inside wardrobes and drawers; window sills, frames and the glass inside; radiators; vacuum carpets and mop hard floors, including under where the bed was.
  • Walls: gently clean off marks, then step back – scrubbing matt paint can leave shiny patches that look worse than the scuff.
  • Outside: Shelter says to remove any rubbish that won't fit in your wheelie bins, and leave the garden in a similar state to when you moved in – you don't have to improve it.

Put it back how you found it

Shelter says your landlord can make deductions if you redecorated or made significant changes without consent – painting a wall a different colour is its example – and its advice is to "put the property back how it was when you moved in".

  • Adhesive hooks and strips: remove them slowly the way the maker says – our Command strips guide has the step-by-step.
  • Shelves and screw holes: if you put anything up, check what you agreed with your landlord before filling or painting – a badly matched patch can be worse than a neat hole. Our shelves guide covers asking permission.
  • Painted walls or swapped fittings: if you changed something with permission, check whether you agreed to change it back. If you didn't have permission, ask your landlord what they'd like before you leave.
  • Their furniture: move it back to where it was on the inventory, and find anything you stored away.

What can – and can't – come off your deposit

Shelter says any deductions "must be reasonable and relate to money lost by the landlord". From Shelter's deductions guide and its cleaning, gardening and decorating page (both checked 29 October 2026):

Usually can be deductedUsually can't be deducted
Unpaid rentFair wear and tear – Shelter's examples are small scuffs on walls, faded curtains or furniture, and worn flooring or frayed carpets
Damage beyond fair wear and tear, and missing itemsRedecorating just because it's due – even if the landlord redecorates
Cleaning, if you left it dirtier than at check-inProfessional cleaning you were made to pay for – Shelter says that clause isn't fair and fees like this are banned
Changes made without consent, such as walls painted a different colourMaking the place better than it was – you don't have to improve it
  • "Clean to a professional standard"? Shelter says that clause is only fair if the home was professionally cleaned before you moved in.
  • Repainting: normal scuffs after years of living are wear and tear. Holes, paint pulled off, or a colour you chose without permission are more likely to be deducted.
  • Carpets: Shelter lists worn or frayed carpets as fair wear and tear. Stains, burns or pet damage are more likely to count as damage – Shelter's pet damage page says damage caused by a pet beyond fair wear and tear can be deducted. How much depends on the carpet's age and condition; not verified beyond Shelter's guidance.

Evidence: the bit that actually wins disputes

A dispute service looks at the evidence from both sides, so the tenant with dated photos and a signed inventory is in a much stronger position.

  • Photos and video of every room on your last day – wide shots, then close-ups of the oven, hob, fridge, bathroom, carpets and any marks. Make sure the date is saved in the file.
  • Your check-in inventory, plus any emails where you reported problems during the tenancy (so a landlord can't blame you for, say, a leak you reported).
  • Receipts for anything you cleaned or replaced.
  • Meter readings and the key return, with dates.

Keep all of this until the deposit has been returned in full or the dispute is decided.

Getting the deposit back – and disputing deductions

  1. Ask in writing. Shelter suggests writing to your landlord or agent if it's more than a week since you moved out. Email is fine – keep a copy.
  2. Agree the amount. GOV.UK says your landlord must return your deposit within 10 days of you both agreeing how much you'll get back.
  3. If they don't reply. Shelter says that with a custodial scheme (where the scheme holds the money) you can ask the scheme for a full refund if it's been at least two weeks since the tenancy ended and either you can't contact the landlord or they haven't replied to your written request within two weeks. With an insurance-based scheme, contact the scheme if the landlord doesn't reply or refund within 10 days of you asking.
  4. If you disagree with deductions. All three schemes offer a free dispute resolution service. GOV.UK says it's optional – you and your landlord both have to agree to use it – and the decision is final. There may be a time limit for raising a dispute, so contact your scheme as soon as you can.
  5. If the deposit was never protected, GOV.UK says you can apply to your local county court – write to the landlord and any agent first. Get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice before you do.

FAQ

Can my landlord charge me for repainting the walls?

Not just because the walls are due a repaint – Shelter says that's fair wear and tear, even if the landlord redecorates. They may be able to deduct for damage, or for a colour you painted without permission.

Can my landlord charge for carpet cleaning?

Shelter says a clause making you pay for professional cleaning isn't fair, and you only need to leave the home as clean as when you moved in. If the carpets are dirtier or stained compared with the inventory, a reasonable deduction is possible; worn or frayed carpets are fair wear and tear.

Do I have to get the flat professionally cleaned when I move out?

No. Shelter says professional cleaning is optional. A "professional standard" clause is only fair if it was professionally cleaned before you moved in.

How long does my landlord have to return my deposit?

GOV.UK says within 10 days of you both agreeing how much you'll get back. If you can't agree, the scheme keeps it protected while the dispute is sorted out.

What's the deposit dispute service?

A free service run by each of the three government-approved schemes. It looks at the evidence from you and your landlord and decides how the deposit is split. Both sides have to agree to use it, and the decision is final.
