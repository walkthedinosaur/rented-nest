Short answer: the best stocking fillers for someone who rents are small, useful and easy to keep – things that live in a pocket, a drawer or on a windowsill, need nothing fixed to a wall, and get used up or used every day. Think hand warmers, flameless tea lights, fridge clips, a phone stand, a pocket card game or a good hot chocolate. Pick a budget per item (say under £5 or under £10), and check the current price before you buy – small items often creep up once delivery is added.

How to fill a stocking without overspending

  • Set a total, not just a per-item limit. Five "cheap" things soon add up. Decide the total first, then split it.
  • Watch delivery costs. A tiny item with its own delivery charge can double in price. Group small orders together or pick them up in town.
  • Go useful over novelty. In a small flat, a gimmick that has to be stored is a burden, not a gift.
  • Mind the batteries. GOV.UK's product safety campaign warns about the risk to children from swallowing button batteries and magnets – worth remembering if the stocking is going to a house with young children visiting.
  • Real candles? London Fire Brigade suggests battery-powered LED candles instead, and never leaving candles unattended – one more reason the LED tea lights above make sense in a rented flat.
  • Order early. Small items sell out in December, and delivery slows in the last fortnight before Christmas.

Frequently asked questions

What are good stocking fillers for adults in the UK?

Small things they'll use up or use every day: hand cream, hand warmers, a good hot chocolate, a pocket game, a phone stand or LED tea lights. For someone who rents, avoid anything that needs fixing to a wall or takes up much space.

How much should I spend on stocking fillers?

There's no rule – agree it with your household. Many people set a total for the whole stocking and aim for a few items under £5 or £10 each. Always check the current price and delivery before you buy.

Are candles a good stocking filler for a rented flat?

Some tenancies and house shares don't allow real candles, and fire services advise flameless LED candles instead. LED tea lights are the safer gift.
