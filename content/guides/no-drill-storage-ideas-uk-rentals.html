Most tenancy agreements restrict drilling, and filling holes at the end of a tenancy eats time and deposit. These ideas add real storage without a single screw. Check your own agreement first – some landlords are happy to approve shelves if you ask in writing. Want the room to look better as well as tidier? See our small living room ideas for renters.

Walls and doors

1. Over-door hooks and racks

They sit over the top of the door with no fixings. Check the hook depth against your door thickness, and look for a felt or rubber backing so they don't scuff the paint.

2. Over-door shoe or pantry organisers

Clear-pocket hanging organisers turn the back of a door into storage for shoes, cleaning kit or snacks.

3. Removable adhesive hooks

Good for keys, utensils and light frames. They can lift paint, especially on fresh or cheap emulsion, so test one somewhere hidden and follow the removal instructions exactly.

Light items only

4. Picture ledges that lean

A long, narrow shelf stood on a sideboard or the floor gives you a gallery wall with no nails.

Floors and corners

5. Ladder shelves

Leaning ladder shelves use height without fixing to the wall. In homes with children or pets, use the anti-tip strap only if your landlord agrees.

6. Tension-pole shelving

A pole pressed between floor and ceiling holds shelves in an awkward corner or above a toilet.

7. Slim rolling trolleys

Narrow trolleys slide into the gap beside the fridge or washing machine and roll out when you need them.

Bigger buys

A canvas wardrobe or covered clothes rail

When there's no built-in wardrobe, a freestanding frame with a zip-up fabric cover keeps clothes dust-free and folds down for the move. Check the height against your longest coats. Usually under £100 at the time of writing – check current price.

A tall freestanding bookcase or cube unit

Tall shelving uses the height of the room instead of the floor, and nothing has to be screwed to the wall. Many come with an anti-tip strap – only fit it if your landlord agrees. Typically around £50–£150 at the time of writing – check current price.

An upholstered storage bench

Somewhere to sit while you put your shoes on, with a lift-up lid for boots, bags or spare bedding. Works at the end of a bed too. Typically around £60–£160 at the time of writing – check current price.

A kitchen trolley with drawers

A proper worktop on wheels adds prep space and storage to a tiny rented kitchen, and goes with you when you leave. Look for lockable castors. Typically around £40–£110 at the time of writing – check current price.
