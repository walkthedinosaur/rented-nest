The short answer: for children who ride, the best presents are useful kit in fun colours – riding gloves, pony socks, a grooming kit of their own. For children who don't ride (yet), it's model horses, toy ponies and hobby horses, or lessons at a local riding school. Leave riding hats and body protectors off the surprise list: a child's hat needs measuring and fitting in person, so give a gift card instead.

Below are 16 ideas grouped by budget. A couple of tips first: ask a parent or riding instructor about sizes and what the child already has, and check whether toys are suitable for the child's age – the retailer listing usually says.

Before you buy: hats need fitting, not wrapping

A riding hat is the most important thing a young rider wears. The British Horse Society advises having a hat fitted by someone with British Equestrian Trade Association (BETA) training, and points out that hats designed for children can be a different shape from adult hats, even from the same brand. It also advises against second-hand hats, because damage can't be seen with the naked eye (checked 31 October 2026).

That rules hats out as a surprise present – and the same goes for body protectors and long boots, where fit matters just as much. If a child needs new safety kit, a gift card for a shop with trained fitters is the kind present, and a trip to get fitted can be part of the treat.

16. Riding lessons

For a pony-mad child who doesn't ride yet, a lesson voucher from a local riding school is hard to beat. The British Horse Society has a find a riding centre search that includes BHS Approved Centres. We haven't checked lesson prices – ask the school.

A gift for the pony? Ask the owner before buying treats or licks – some ponies are on restricted diets, and their vet's advice comes first.
