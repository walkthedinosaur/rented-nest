The short answer: stand your horse square and use a soft tape to measure from the centre of the chest, around the widest part of the shoulder, along the side to the back of the quarters (just past the point of the buttock). That measurement, in feet and inches, is your UK rug size. Rugs come in 3-inch steps, and if your horse falls between two sizes, most retailers suggest going up. Then check the brand's own size chart – sizing isn't the same from brand to brand – and check the fit once the rug is on.

Here's how to take the measurement, how UK sizes compare with the centimetre sizes on European rugs, and what to look for when you try a new rug on.

How to measure your horse, step by step

  1. Get a helper and a soft tape. A dressmaker's tape is ideal. No tape? Use a length of baling twine or string and measure it afterwards, as The Saddlery Shop suggests.
  2. Stand the horse square on level ground, with their head straight rather than turned towards you.
  3. Start at the centre of the chest. The British Horse Society says to measure from there, around the widest part of the shoulder, then along the side of the body to the buttock.
  4. Finish just past the point of the buttock – where you want the rug to end. Keep the tape level along the side rather than letting it droop.
  5. Write it down in feet and inches. Round up to the next 3-inch size if you're between two (see below).

Already have a rug that fits well? WeatherBeeta's guide (on VioVet's blog) says you can lay it flat and measure from the centre of the chest to the end of the rug instead.

UK sizes vs European cm sizes

This is where most sizing mix-ups happen. There are two common systems, and they don't measure the same length:

  • UK (and US) sizes are the length from the centre of the chest to the back of the quarters, in 3-inch steps. UK sizes are written in feet and inches (6'0, 6'3); US sizes in inches (72", 75").
  • European sizes are in 5cm steps, and some brands measure them differently: WeatherBeeta's guide and Shires both describe the European measurement as back length, from the withers to the tail.

Because of this, there's no exact conversion that works for every brand. As one example, this is the size chart Kingdom Equestrian publishes for Imperial Riding rugs (checked 31 October 2026). Treat it as an illustration, not a rule for other brands:

Between sizes, stable rugs and turnout rugs

Between sizes? WeatherBeeta's guide and The Saddlery Shop both recommend going up a size, because a rug that's too small is more likely to rub.

Turnout rugs: The Saddlery Shop suggests that your measurement gives the size for a stable rug, and to add an extra 3 inches for a turnout rug, especially one with a neck cover – a turnout needs room for the horse to move, graze and roll. This is one retailer's advice, so compare it with the size guide for the rug you're buying.

Ponies: small rugs are sold in both systems – GS Equestrian's filters, for example, list pony sizes in centimetres as well as in feet and inches from 2'6. Check which one a listing uses before you order.

How to check the fit

The tape gets you close; the fit check tells you whether it's right. Shires suggests trying a new rug over a clean sheet first, and keeping the tags on, so it's still returnable if it doesn't fit. Then check:

  • Withers, shoulders and chest – the BHS says to pay particular attention here. The rug should sit in front of the withers, not behind them (The Saddlery Shop warns a rug sitting behind the withers can pull back and rub). You should be able to slide a flat hand down the front of the rug at the chest.
  • Length – good cover over the quarters without hanging far past the top of the tail.
  • Neck – a neck cover that's too big hangs back and presses on the shoulders.
  • Belly and leg straps – about a hand's width between each strap and the horse (Shires and The Saddlery Shop). Secure but not overly tight, in the BHS's words.
  • Movement – look at the fit with the horse standing, walking and with their head down to graze.

Once it's in use, the BHS recommends taking the rug off every day to check for rubbing and discomfort, and refitting or changing it if needed – an ill-fitting rug can cause injury. If you spot rubs or sore patches, or you're unsure whether your horse needs a rug at all, speak to your vet.
