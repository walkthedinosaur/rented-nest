The short answer: dress in thin layers rather than one bulky coat. Start with a close-fitting base layer that isn't cotton, add a fleece or gilet, and finish with a waterproof, windproof, breathable riding jacket. Wear thermal riding tights or breeches (or a thin layer under your usual pair), proper winter riding gloves with grip, warm socks that still let your boots fit, and hi-vis whenever you ride out. Your riding hat stays exactly as it is – keep the fit right and don't pack anything under it that changes how it sits.

Winter riding is mostly about staying warm without losing movement or grip. Here's how to put an outfit together from the top half down.

Layer up: base, mid and outer

Every guide we read agrees on the same three-layer system. Your Horse's winter clothing guide sets it out like this:

  • Base layer – close-fitting and breathable, in merino, bamboo or a synthetic fabric. Your Horse says it's "best to avoid cotton" because it soaks up moisture – and once you've sweated through mucking out, damp cotton gets cold fast.
  • Mid layer – a fleece, or a down or synthetic-filled gilet or jacket. It should fit well enough that it doesn't restrict your arms in the saddle.
  • Outer layer – waterproof, windproof and breathable, ideally with a removable or tuck-away hood and rear vents so it sits over the saddle.

The advantage of layers is that you can strip one off after the warm-up or while doing yard jobs, then put it back on for the hack home. Horse & Hound's winter tips add a high neck, vents and elasticated cuffs to the wish-list for a riding coat, plus a fleecy snood for your neck.

Legs: thermal tights, breeches and waterproofs

For your legs you have three options, and you can combine them: thermal riding tights or breeches with a brushed or fleece lining; your usual jodhpurs or breeches with a thin thermal layer underneath (Horse & Hound suggests thermal leggings or thick tights); or waterproof over-trousers on top for wet hacks and yard work. Your Horse recommends the full stack on the coldest days – base layer, winter breeches, then waterproofs. If you're unsure which style of riding trousers to get, see jodhpurs vs breeches.

Hands, feet and head

  • Gloves – you need warmth without losing feel on the reins. Insulated riding gloves (often Thinsulate-lined) with grippy palms are the usual answer. Horse & Hound's tip is woolly fingerless gloves over the top on bitter days, and hand warmers in your pockets.
  • Feet – warm socks, but only as many as still let your foot fit comfortably in your boot; Horse & Hound makes the same point. Squashed toes get colder, and a boot that's too tight is harder to slip out of the stirrup. For the yard, waterproof insulated yard boots with non-slip soles help in mud and frost.
  • Head and ears – your riding hat must fit and be fastened properly; the BHS says it's "essential that your hat is secured and fits properly". Don't wear a bobble hat or thick beanie under it. If your ears suffer, Horse & Hound mentions fleece or sheepskin ear warmers that attach to the hat harness – if you try something like this, check it doesn't change how the hat sits, and ask a trained hat fitter if in doubt. A snood or neck warmer covers the gap between collar and hat.

Short days: hi-vis and lights

Winter means low sun, grey afternoons and early dusk. The BHS hi-vis page (checked 21 November 2026, last reviewed 28 August 2026) says:

  • The BHS "always recommends wearing hi-vis clothing and lights" when riding, whatever the time of day, season or weather.
  • As a minimum: a hi-vis jacket or tabard for the rider and leg bands on the horse.
  • It recommends LED lights wherever possible, placed to show your width – for example on the rider's shoulders and the horse's flanks.
  • The BHS would not recommend riding out at night or in poor visibility, in line with Rule 51 of the Highway Code.
  • For leisure riders it points to hi-vis certified to EN 17353:2020 (accessories such as hat bands included); EN ISO 20471 applies to professional, employer-issued kit.
  • Pick colours that stand out against the background – some colours blend into hedgerows at certain times of year.

Older blog posts (including some retailer guides) quote earlier hi-vis standards. Go by the BHS page and the label on the garment.

A simple winter riding checklist

  • Close-fitting non-cotton base layer
  • Fleece or gilet you can move in
  • Waterproof, breathable riding jacket with vents
  • Thermal riding tights/breeches, or a thin layer underneath
  • Insulated riding gloves with grip
  • Warm socks that still fit in your boots; insulated yard boots for chores
  • Your correctly fitted riding hat – nothing bulky underneath
  • Hi-vis for you and your horse, plus lights, whenever you ride out

Your horse needs thinking about too – see horse rug weights explained. For questions about whether a particular horse needs rugging or extra feed in cold weather, speak to your vet.
