The short answer: the best gifts for riders are the things that wear out, get lost or make cold yard mornings easier – riding and yard gloves, good socks, grooming brushes, a hoof pick for every pocket, and treats or stable toys for the horse. If you want to give safety kit like a riding hat or body protector, give a gift card instead: they need to be fitted in person, and second-hand hats aren't a good idea.

Here are 16 ideas sorted by price, with the one big "don't" first. A few quick rules make any horsey present more useful: buy consumables (gloves, socks, brushes) rather than ornaments, check sizes with someone who knows, and keep the receipt – colour and brand loyalties run deep at most yards. If the gift is for the horse rather than the rider, ask the owner before buying any food or treats.

Before you buy: don't gift a hat as a surprise

A riding hat is the most important bit of kit a rider owns, which makes it tempting as a present. But the British Horse Society recommends having a new hat fitted by someone with British Equestrian Trade Association (BETA) training, and notes that children's hats can be a different shape from the same brand's adult hats. It also advises against buying second-hand hats, because damage can't be seen with the naked eye, and says to replace a hat after a fall (checked 30 October 2026).

So: if the rider in your life needs a new hat or body protector, a gift card to a shop with trained fitters is the practical present. The same goes for anything where size and fit really matter, like long boots.
