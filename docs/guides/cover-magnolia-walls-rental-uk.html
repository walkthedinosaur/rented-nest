How to cover magnolia walls in a rental (UK)

Stuck with magnolia walls in a rented flat? Renter-friendly ways to make them look better without painting – lighting, colour, rugs and leaning art – and how to ask your landlord about painting.

Short answer: you usually can't paint over magnolia without your landlord's say-so, but you don't need to. Warmer lighting, strong colour in textiles, a big rug and large leaned art take the eye off the walls – and all of it comes with you when you move. If you'd still like to paint, ask your landlord in writing and offer to paint it back before you leave.

Magnolia and other pale creams are popular with landlords because they're cheap, quick to touch up between tenants and suit most furniture. The downside is that they can look flat, yellow or tired, especially under a cold ceiling light. Here's how to work with them.

First: can you paint the walls?

Check your tenancy agreement for clauses about decorating or "alterations". Shelter's advice on home improvements in a rented home is not to make improvements without your landlord's permission, and to get their agreement in writing before you start. It lists painting and decorating as an example, and notes that some landlords agree as long as you put things back the way they were when you leave.

  • Ask by email, with the colour and which rooms. A short message gives you a dated record.
  • Offer to paint it back to the original colour at the end of the tenancy, or ask whether they'd keep a neutral new colour.
  • Keep the reply with your inventory. Without permission, Shelter warns a landlord may ask you to change things back or try to keep some of your deposit.

If the answer is no – or you'd rather not risk it – everything below works without touching the walls. For the rest of the flat, see how to make a rented flat feel like home without decorating.

Change the light, not the paint

Magnolia looks its most yellow under one bright overhead bulb. Several lower lights spread around the room soften it and make the walls read as warm rather than dingy.

A floor lamp in the darkest corner

Lighting a corner pushes the walls back visually and gives a small room more depth. A fabric or paper shade gives a gentler glow on cream walls than a bare bulb.

Put the colour in textiles

Cream walls are a neutral background, so let colour come from things you own. Pick one or two colours that look good next to warm cream – deep green, navy, terracotta, ochre and rust tend to work – and repeat them around the room.

A large rug in a strong colour

A rug is the biggest area of colour you can add without paint. It pulls the eye down and away from the walls. Use a non-slip underlay on carpet or hard floors.

Peel-off wallpaper: read this first

Peel-and-stick wallpaper is sold as "renter friendly", but it has the same problem as adhesive strips: it sticks to the paint, and when you peel it off, the paint can come with it. Fresh, cheap or poorly prepared paint is the most at risk.

  • Ask your landlord first, as for painting, and keep their answer in writing.
  • Test a small patch somewhere hidden for a couple of weeks, then peel it slowly and check the paint.
  • Keep it small. An alcove, the back of a bookcase or a headboard area is far less risky than a whole wall.
  • Avoid damp walls. On cold outside walls that get condensation, adhesive may fail and can trap moisture. If walls are often wet, see condensation and damp in a rented home.

FAQ

Can my landlord charge me for painting the walls?

If you paint without permission and leave it that way, your landlord may ask you to put it back or try to deduct the cost from your deposit. Your deposit should be protected in a government-approved scheme, and if you can't agree on a deduction, each scheme offers a free dispute service. See GOV.UK on tenancy deposit protection.

What colours go with magnolia?

Warm, deeper colours usually sit better with cream than cool greys or bright white, which can make magnolia look more yellow. Try a cushion or throw in the room before you buy anything big.

Is it worth asking the landlord to repaint?

If the walls are marked or tired when you move in, note it on your inventory with photos. That protects your deposit, and it's a natural moment to ask whether the landlord plans to redecorate.
