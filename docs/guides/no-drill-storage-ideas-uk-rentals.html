15 no-drill storage ideas for UK rentals

Storage that adds space without screws, holes or a lost deposit – for small UK flats and house shares.

Updated October 2026

Most tenancy agreements restrict drilling, and filling holes at the end of a tenancy eats time and deposit. These ideas add real storage without a single screw. Check your own agreement first – some landlords are happy to approve shelves if you ask in writing.

Walls and doors

1. Over-door hooks and racks

They sit over the top of the door with no fixings. Check the hook depth against your door thickness, and look for a felt or rubber backing so they don't scuff the paint.

HallwaysBedroomsBathrooms

2. Over-door shoe or pantry organisers

Clear-pocket hanging organisers turn the back of a door into storage for shoes, cleaning kit or snacks.

CupboardsKitchens

3. Removable adhesive hooks

Good for keys, utensils and light frames. They can lift paint, especially on fresh or cheap emulsion, so test one somewhere hidden and follow the removal instructions exactly.

Light items only

4. Picture ledges that lean

A long, narrow shelf stood on a sideboard or the floor gives you a gallery wall with no nails.

Living rooms

Floors and corners

5. Ladder shelves

Leaning ladder shelves use height without fixing to the wall. In homes with children or pets, use the anti-tip strap only if your landlord agrees.

Small living rooms

6. Tension-pole shelving

A pole pressed between floor and ceiling holds shelves in an awkward corner or above a toilet.

BathroomsCorners

7. Slim rolling trolleys

Narrow trolleys slide into the gap beside the fridge or washing machine and roll out when you need them.

Kitchens

8. Freestanding clothes rails

When the wardrobe runs out, an open rail doubles as display. Choose one with a shelf underneath for shoes.

Bedrooms

Under and inside

9. Bed risers and under-bed boxes

Risers lift the bed a few inches so boxes fit underneath. Measure your clearance before you buy boxes.

Bedrooms

10. Shelf risers inside cupboards

Small fold-out shelves double the usable space in kitchen cupboards without touching the carcass.

Kitchens

11. Under-shelf baskets

Wire baskets that slide onto an existing shelf add a second layer for wraps, foil or tea towels.

KitchensWardrobes

12. Tension rods inside cupboards

A short rod under the sink holds spray bottles by their triggers, freeing the floor of the cupboard.

KitchensBathrooms

Furniture that works twice

13. Storage ottomans and benches

Seating with a lift-up lid hides blankets, toys or shoes.

Living roomsHallways

14. Cube units on their side

A low cube unit can be a TV bench, a room divider or a headboard shelf, and moves with you.

Every room

15. Nesting side tables

Two or three tables that tuck inside each other give surface space when guests arrive and almost none when they leave.

Small living rooms

Before you move out, photograph every wall and door you used, so you have a record if the check-out report raises anything.
