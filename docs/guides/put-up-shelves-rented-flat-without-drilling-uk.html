Can I put shelves up in a rented flat without drilling? (UK)

Short answer: not without asking. Drilling holes for shelves is a change to the property, so check your tenancy agreement and get your landlord's agreement in writing first. Hackney Council's advice to private tenants puts it plainly: "you can't just put up shelves without permission." If the answer is no – or you'd rather not risk your deposit – freestanding, tension-pole and over-door shelving gives you the storage with nothing to fill when you leave.

Can I put shelves up in a rented flat?

Start with your tenancy agreement. Look for clauses about "alterations", "fixtures", fixing things to walls or making holes. Some ban it outright; many say you need the landlord's consent; some say nothing.

  • Banned: use the no-drill options below.
  • Needs consent, or not mentioned: ask. Shelter's advice on changes to a rented home is not to make them without your landlord's permission and to get their agreement in writing before you start. It also says a letting agent usually can't agree to improvements without the landlord being involved.
  • Already fitted shelves? If the landlord's shelves are loose or the brackets are failing, report that as a repair rather than refixing them yourself.

Without permission, Shelter warns a landlord might ask you to change things back or keep your deposit. Hackney Council adds the general rule: return the property in the condition you found it, allowing for some unavoidable wear and tear – "the property isn't yours."

How to ask your landlord (and what to say)

A short email gives you a dated record. Be specific – landlords say yes more easily when they know exactly what's going on the wall.

Hi [name], I'd like to put up two small shelves in the living room, on the wall beside the window, using wall plugs and screws. Before I move out I'll take them down, fill and sand the holes, and touch up the paint to match – or leave the shelves if you'd prefer. Are you happy for me to do this? Thanks, [your name]

  • Offer to put it back. Shelter says some landlords agree as long as you return things to how they were when you leave, so make that clear from the start.
  • Ask about the wall. Your landlord may know whether it's plasterboard, solid brick, or has pipes or cables running through it.
  • Keep the reply with your inventory and check-in photos.

What counts as damage: holes, filler and your deposit

Your deposit should be protected in a government-approved scheme. Shelter says deductions must be reasonable and relate to money the landlord has lost, and landlords can't deduct for fair wear and tear – but it's damage beyond normal use that you're responsible for, and holes you made without permission may well be counted as damage.

  • Photograph the walls when you move in, and again after you've made good.
  • Only fill and paint with agreement, using matching paint – a patch that doesn't match can look worse than a small hole.
  • If you can't agree a deduction, GOV.UK says each deposit scheme offers a free dispute resolution service. Both sides have to agree to use it, and the decision is final.

Adhesive shelves – use with care

Stick-on shelves and shower caddies are sold as "no drilling", but adhesive can lift paint, and a lot of letting agents treat that as damage. If your tenancy allows it, keep them for very light items, test on a hidden spot first, follow the maker's instructions, and remove them slowly well before your check-out. Check the weight rating, especially for anything that holds wet bottles.

FAQ

Do I need permission to put shelves up in a rented flat?

Usually, yes. Check your tenancy agreement and ask your landlord in writing before drilling. Hackney Council's advice is that you can't just put up shelves without permission, and Shelter says to get any agreement in writing.

Can my landlord charge me for holes in the wall?

If you made holes without permission and didn't make good, they may try to deduct the cost from your deposit. Deductions must be reasonable, and if you disagree, your deposit scheme's free dispute service can decide.

Can I use Command strips for shelves?

Only for very light items, and only if your tenancy allows adhesive fixings – they can take paint off when removed. Freestanding shelving is safer for your deposit. See Command strips in a rented flat.

What's the best shelving for a rented flat?

Freestanding pieces – a ladder shelf, a tall bookcase or cube unit, or a tension-pole corner unit – because they need no fixings and move with you.
