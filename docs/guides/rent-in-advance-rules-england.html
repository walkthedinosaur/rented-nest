How much rent in advance can a landlord ask for? (England, 2026)

Since 1 May 2026 landlords and agents in England can't take rent before you sign, and can ask for no more than one month's rent up front after signing. What to say if you're asked for six months, options if you have no payslip, and how to report it.

Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · Covers private renters in England only. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: one month's rent, at most. Since 1 May 2026, a landlord or letting agent in England can't ask for, encourage or accept any rent before the tenancy agreement is signed. Once it's signed, they can ask for the first month's rent before you move in (or up to 28 days' rent if you pay weekly) – and no more. A clause saying you must pay rent in advance after that isn't valid. You can still be asked for a holding deposit (up to one week's rent) and a tenancy deposit (usually up to five weeks' rent), but those are deposits, not rent.

Not in England? Different rules apply – see private residential tenancies in Scotland, Renting Homes in Wales and private renting in Northern Ireland.

The rules: before you sign, after you sign, after you move in

From GOV.UK's rent in advance guidance (updated 8 May 2026) and Shelter's rent in advance page (updated 25 September 2026), both checked 19 November 2026:

  • Before everyone has signed: no rent at all. The landlord or agent can't ask for it, and if you offer, they must not accept it. Deposits can be asked for at this stage.
  • After signing, before the tenancy starts: they can ask for the first month's rent if you pay monthly, or up to the first 28 days' rent if you pay more often (for example weekly). They must not demand more, and you can refuse to pay more.
  • Once the tenancy has started: rent is due on the dates in your agreement. Terms that require rent in advance aren't valid.
  • No disguised fees: Shelter says landlords and agents must not hide extra fees by calling them rent in advance.

Who it covers: private assured tenancies in England where the agreement was signed on or after 1 May 2026. Agreements signed before that date, and rent paid before it, aren't covered. Social and supported housing are excluded from these rules.

The same rules sit alongside the other changes from 1 May 2026 – see the Renters' Rights Act: what changed.

Asked for three or six months up front? What to say

If an agent or landlord asks for more than one month's rent before you move in – or for any rent before you've signed – that's against the rules for a new private tenancy in England. You don't have to argue on the spot. Ask for the request in writing, then reply politely. Keep the advert, messages and any payment request.

Subject: Rent in advance – [property address]

Dear [agent or landlord's name],

Thank you for offering me the tenancy at [address]. You've asked me to pay [£amount / X months' rent] in advance.

My understanding of the Renters' Rights Act rules for tenancies signed from 1 May 2026 is that rent can't be taken before the tenancy agreement is signed, and that after signing no more than the first month's rent can be requested before the tenancy starts.

I'm happy to pay the holding deposit, the tenancy deposit and the first month's rent once the agreement is signed. Please could you confirm the amounts in writing?

Kind regards,
[Your name]
[Date]

If they insist, or say the offer depends on paying more, that's the point to get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice and think about reporting it (below).

No payslip? Savings, self-employed or starting a new job

Offering six or twelve months' rent up front used to be the usual way round a missing payslip. For new tenancies in England that's mostly gone, because landlords can't ask for or take more than a month before you move in. What you can do instead:

  • Ask about referencing before you apply. It's usually the agent's or referencing company's checklist, not the law, that asks for payslips. Ask whether they accept bank statements showing savings, a letter confirming a job offer, self-employed accounts or a tax return.
  • Count all your income. If you get benefits, GOV.UK says a landlord who checks affordability must count benefit income the same way as other income – see can a landlord refuse tenants on benefits?
  • A guarantor. Many agents accept one. We haven't found GOV.UK guidance on how the new rules treat guarantor requirements, so this is not verified – if one is asked for, check with Shelter or Citizens Advice.
  • References. A reference from your current landlord showing you've paid on time can matter more now that a big upfront payment isn't an option.
  • Private landlords letting directly may be more flexible about evidence than large agents – but the same one-month rule applies to them.

Once your tenancy has started, GOV.UK's guidance says you can choose to pay rent early if you want to. It just can't be a condition of getting the flat.

Offering more rent to win the flat (the bidding ban)

The same changes stop bidding wars. GOV.UK's overview for tenants (checked 19 November 2026) says written adverts and offers must state a specific rent, and landlords can't accept or encourage offers above the advertised rent. Shelter says the ban on taking rent before signing is also meant to stop rent bidding.

  • If an agent says "other people are offering more" or asks for your "best offer", keep a copy of the message and the advert showing the price.
  • Don't offer extra months up front to beat other applicants – before signing they mustn't accept it, and after signing they can't take more than a month before you move in.

Reporting it and getting money back

From GOV.UK's guidance for local authorities (checked 19 November 2026):

  1. Report it to your council. Councils' trading standards teams enforce these rules (other council teams may too), and they can investigate.
  2. Penalties: the council can issue a civil penalty of up to £5,000 for a first breach. A repeat within five years can lead to a fine of up to £30,000 or prosecution.
  3. Getting the money back: where a penalty is issued, the council can require the landlord or agent to repay the payment that was taken illegally.

Keep everything: the advert, emails or texts asking for the money, bank transfer records and the signed agreement with its date. If you've already paid and want it back, ask Shelter or Citizens Advice about the best route for your situation before you write to the landlord.

FAQ

Can a landlord ask for six months' rent in advance in 2026?

Not for a new private tenancy in England signed on or after 1 May 2026. They can ask for the first month's rent after you sign and before you move in, and no more.

Is a holding deposit the same as rent in advance?

No. A holding deposit reserves the flat while checks are done and is capped at one week's rent. The tenancy deposit is capped at five weeks' rent (six weeks if the annual rent is £50,000 or more) and must be protected in a government scheme – see my deposit isn't protected.

I signed before 1 May 2026 and paid six months up front. Can I get it back?

These rules don't apply to agreements signed, or rent paid, before 1 May 2026. If the money was meant as security rather than rent, it may count as a deposit – our "advance payment" section explains how to tell, and Shelter or Citizens Advice can look at your paperwork.

Can I choose to pay my rent early?

Yes, once the tenancy has started, GOV.UK's guidance says you can choose to pay rent early. A landlord just can't make it a condition.

Does this apply to lodgers or council tenants?

The rules are for private assured tenancies. Social and supported housing are excluded, and lodgers aren't assured tenants. Ask Shelter or Citizens Advice if you're not sure what kind of tenancy you have.
