How to make a rented flat feel like home (without decorating)

Make a rented flat feel like yours without painting or drilling: light, textiles, plants, scent and leaned art – what you're allowed to change, a first-weekend list and bigger buys you take with you.

Short answer: change what you bring, not what you fix. Warm light, soft textiles, plants, your own bedding and the things that make a place smell and sound like yours do most of the work – and none of them touch the walls, so none of them should touch your deposit. Ask your landlord in writing before anything that's fixed, like paint, shelves or new curtain poles.

This guide is the whole-flat plan. For one room or one problem, see our guides to small living room ideas for renters, covering magnolia walls and no-drill storage.

First, what you can and can't change

Anything you can pick up and take with you is yours to change: lamps, rugs, cushions, plants, art you lean rather than hang, and furniture. Anything fixed – paint colours, new holes, swapped light fittings – needs your landlord's say-so. Shelter says a landlord can keep some of your deposit if you redecorate or make significant changes without consent, and gives painting walls a different colour as an example. It also says a landlord can't keep any of your deposit for fair wear and tear.

FAQ

Can I hang pictures in a rented flat?

Check your tenancy agreement and ask your landlord. Adhesive strips are often allowed but can lift paint – see can you use Command strips in a rented flat. Leaning frames avoids the question altogether.

Can I paint a rented flat?

Only with your landlord's agreement, ideally in writing. Shelter gives painting walls a different colour as an example of a change that could lead to deposit deductions without consent. More in our magnolia walls guide.

How do I make a rented flat feel cosy in winter?

Warm lamps, a heavy throw and a rug help, but so does keeping the heat in. See how to stay warm in a cold rented flat.
