Christmas decorations in a rented flat – without damaging the walls (UK)

Yes, you can decorate a rented flat for Christmas. Hang, clip, lean or stand things instead of sticking or nailing them – no-nail ideas for fairy lights, wreaths and a small-flat tree, plus fairy light safety and taking it all down in January.

Short answer: yes, you can decorate a rented flat for Christmas. The trick is to use things that hang, clip, lean or stand rather than things that stick or nail, and to check your tenancy agreement for anything about adhesives or fixings first. Do that and your decorations come down in January with nothing left behind – and nothing for your landlord to charge for.

This guide is the no-damage plan for the whole flat: walls, windows, the front door and the tree. For hanging things the rest of the year, see can you use Command strips in a rented flat and our no-drill storage ideas.

What your tenancy (and deposit) means for decorations

Nothing in renting rules stops you putting up a tree or fairy lights. The risk is the walls: nails, screws, Blu-tack and sticky hooks can all leave marks, and marks are what end up as deposit deductions. Fair wear and tear can't be charged for, but new holes or patches of lifted paint usually aren't wear and tear.

  • Read your tenancy agreement for anything about fixings, adhesives or "nothing on the walls". Some agents are stricter than others.
  • Adhesive hooks aren't risk-free. One UK letting agent, CPS Homes, says Command strips "aren't always as damage-free as they claim to be" – there's more on this in our Command strips guide.
  • Not sure? Ask by email, so you have a dated reply to show at check-out.
  • Take a photo of each wall before decorations go up – useful if anything is questioned later. Our move-out deposit checklist explains why dated photos matter.

Hang it, don't stick it

Most flats already have plenty of places to hang decorations from without touching a wall: doors, curtain poles, shelves, banisters and any picture rail or hooks the landlord has already put up.

Over-door hooks

Slip over the top of any internal door to hang a wreath, a garland or stockings. Nothing to screw in, nothing to peel off. Add a strip of felt behind the hook if the door is freshly painted.

Fairy lights with no nails or sticky hooks

Fairy lights are where most tape and nail damage happens. Wrap, drape and weave them instead:

  • Wrap them round a banister, a curtain pole, a headboard, the legs of a shelf or a mirror frame.
  • Drape them along the top of a bookcase or in a glass jar or vase on the windowsill.
  • Hang them from a tension rod or the curtain pole so they fall down the window.
  • Suction-cup hooks work on clean glass. They can leave a ring on the glass (it wipes off), but don't press them onto painted window frames.

Battery or USB fairy lights

No plug means you can put them anywhere – a shelf, a wreath, a jar – without trailing leads across the room or needing a socket nearby. Look for a timer so they switch themselves off.

Fairy light safety (from Electrical Safety First): check lights for damage and loose wires before use; it recommends LED lights over traditional filament ones; don't connect different lighting sets together; don't overload sockets and rely on extension leads or adaptors; and switch lights off before you go to bed or go out. If you're unsure about old lights, replace them from a reputable supplier.

Taking it all down in January

If something did get stuck to a wall, take it off slowly: 3M says to pull the tab straight down, slowly, stretching it along the wall rather than pulling it towards you, and to warm a stubborn one with a hairdryer first. Our Command strips guide has the full method and what to do if paint comes off.

  • Check every wall and frame against your "before" photos while you're packing up.
  • Hoover needles out of the carpet straight away – they work their way in.
  • Box the hooks and clips with the decorations, so next year you're not tempted by the nail drawer.

FAQ

Can I put Christmas decorations up in a rented house in the UK?

Yes. Renting rules don't stop you decorating – the risk is damage to the walls or fittings. Use things that hang, clip, lean or stand, check your tenancy agreement for anything about adhesives, and ask your landlord by email if you're unsure.

How do I hang fairy lights without nails or sticky hooks?

Wrap them round a banister, curtain pole or headboard, drape them along shelves, hang them from a tension rod, or use suction-cup hooks on clean glass. Battery lights can go anywhere with no lead.

Can I use Command strips for Christmas decorations?

Often, but they can still lift paint, and some tenancy agreements say nothing on the walls. Read can you use Command strips in a rented flat before you buy.

How do I hang a wreath on a rented front door?

Use an over-door wreath hanger that hooks over the top of the door – no nail, no screw. In a block of flats, check any building rules about the outside of front doors.
