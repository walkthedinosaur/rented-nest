Short answer: the best gifts for someone moving into their first flat are the useful things nobody buys for themselves – a small toolkit, a surge-protected extension lead, decent pans that fit a tiny kitchen, something cosy for a cold first winter, and storage or decor that needs no drilling. Renters move often and can't fix things to the walls without asking, so pick things that are small, portable and safe for the deposit. Prices change daily, so check the current price before you buy.

Small extras – and what not to buy

A low-light houseplant

Rented flats often face the wrong way. A snake plant, pothos or ZZ plant copes with less light and the odd missed watering.

What not to buy:

  • Anything that has to be fixed to a wall – shelves, mirrors that need screws, curtain poles. They'll need the landlord's permission first.
  • Big furniture before they've measured. Stairs, doorways and tiny rooms rule out a lot.
  • Appliances the flat already has. Ask them to check the inventory before you buy a kettle, toaster or microwave.
  • Lots of ornaments. They'll be packing it all again within a year or two.

FAQ

What's a good housewarming gift for a renter?

Something useful and portable: a toolkit, a surge-protected extension lead, a rug, a heated throw or storage that needs no drilling. Anything that has to be fixed to the walls is best avoided.

What does someone need in their first flat?

Basics for the kitchen, bedding, cleaning kit, a few tools and a way to store things without drilling. Our first flat essentials checklist lists it room by room, plus the checks to do on day one.

Is it okay to give money for a first flat?

Yes – moving costs add up, and money or a gift card lets them buy what the flat is actually missing. Pair it with something small to unwrap if you like.

What's a cheap but useful first flat gift?

A torch, a hygrometer, a door draught excluder, food storage tubs or over-door hooks are all small, useful and usually low-cost. Check the current price before you buy.
