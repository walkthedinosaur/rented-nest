No heating or hot water? What to do when your landlord won't fix the boiler (England)

Left without heating or hot water in a rented home? Whose job the boiler is, how to report it, why you shouldn't withhold rent, when to call the council – and how to keep warm while you wait.

Updated November 2026 · Covers England. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: if you rent privately in England, keeping the boiler, radiators, pipes and hot water working is your landlord's legal job, and you can't sign that away in your tenancy agreement. Report the problem straight away and confirm it in writing. There's no fixed deadline for private landlords, but Shelter says heating and hot water problems are often an emergency and that a "reasonable time" can be shorter in winter. If your landlord drags their feet, contact your council's private housing (environmental health) team. Don't stop paying rent – that puts your home at risk. And if you smell gas, call the National Gas Emergency line on 0800 111 999 first.

Below: what the law says, how to report it, what to do if you've been left without heating for weeks, and how to keep warm safely in the meantime. For everyday ways to heat a cold flat cheaply, see how to stay warm in a cold rented flat.

Is a broken boiler the landlord's responsibility?

Yes. Shelter's guidance for England explains that under section 11 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 a landlord must keep in repair and proper working order the installations that supply heating and hot water – that includes the boiler, central heating, pipes and radiators. The same duty covers the electrics, such as wiring and sockets. Your home must also be fit to live in throughout the tenancy, and a landlord can't use the tenancy agreement to shift these duties onto you.

  • How long can a landlord leave you without heating? For private landlords there's no fixed number of days. Shelter says a landlord has a reasonable time to do repairs, that heating and hot water problems are often an emergency needing urgent attention, and that what's reasonable may be shorter in winter.
  • Social housing is different. Under Awaab's Law, councils and housing associations must investigate and resolve emergency hazards – which can include a broken boiler – within 24 hours, or offer somewhere else to stay. Those fixed timescales don't yet apply to private landlords; extending Awaab's Law to private renting is out for consultation until 18 December 2026.
  • Cold is a recognised hazard. Shelter notes that indoor temperatures below 16°C bring serious health risks for vulnerable people. If someone in your home is elderly, very young, disabled or unwell, say so when you report it.

Report it – then put it in writing

Ring or message your landlord or letting agent the same day – Shelter says report problems immediately – and follow up by email or text so there's a dated record. Say:

  • what isn't working (heating, hot water or both) and since when;
  • any error code on the boiler display or what it's doing;
  • who lives with you and anyone who's especially at risk from the cold;
  • when an engineer can get in.

Keep copies of every message, note every call, and keep receipts for extra costs such as heaters or the extra electricity. If you're compensated later, you'll need that evidence. If an agent manages the property and isn't passing things on, write to the landlord too.

Can you get it fixed yourself and send the bill? Shelter says tenants sometimes pay for an emergency repair and try to reclaim the cost, but there's no guarantee you'll get all or any of it back. Get advice before you do this.

Smell gas? And the gas safety record

If you smell gas, the Health and Safety Executive says call the gas emergency freephone on 0800 111 999, open doors and windows, turn the gas off at the meter if you know how, and don't use any appliance you think is unsafe.

  • Annual check: your landlord must get a Gas Safe registered engineer to check gas appliances every 12 months (HSE).
  • Your copy: existing tenants should get a copy of the gas safety record within 28 days of the check, and new tenants before they move in. If the boiler has failed and you've never seen one, ask for it in the same email.
  • Carbon monoxide alarms: in England, landlords must provide a CO alarm in any room used as living accommodation that has a fixed combustion appliance, such as a gas boiler (gas cookers excepted).

Landlord won't fix it? Council, rent and compensation

  • Chase in writing and give a clear date you expect an engineer by.
  • Contact the council. Ask for the private sector housing or environmental health team. Shelter explains that a council must take action if it finds a serious hazard, and can serve an improvement notice or take emergency action. (Council tenants complain to their landlord and then the Housing Ombudsman instead.)
  • Can I withhold rent? Don't. Shelter warns it's a risk because the landlord can start possession proceedings for rent arrears – and serious arrears are one of the legal reasons a landlord can use to evict. Keep paying, and get advice.
  • Can I get a rent reduction? Only if your landlord agrees it, in writing. Otherwise Shelter says you may be able to claim compensation for disrepair – which is claimed, not taken off the rent yourself. Your receipts and log are the evidence.
  • Worried about being evicted for complaining? Since 1 May 2026, private landlords in England can't use "no fault" section 21 notices – they need a legal ground and a court hearing. See our Renters' Rights Act guide.

For advice on your own situation, contact Shelter, Citizens Advice or your council. Rules differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Keeping warm while you wait

If the repair takes days, a plug-in heater for the room you use most, plus heat that warms you rather than the room, gets you through. Keep receipts: extra heating costs are the kind of thing to log in case you claim later. To compare running costs at today's energy price cap, use our heating and drying cost calculator.

Heater safety (Hertfordshire Fire and Rescue): keep heaters at least a metre from curtains, furniture, bedding and clothes; never use one to dry washing; stand it on a flat surface and choose one with tip-over protection; don't overload sockets or use coiled cable drums; and switch it off when you leave the room or go to bed.

FAQ

How long can a landlord leave you without heating or hot water?

For private renters there's no set number of days – the landlord must repair it within a reasonable time, and Shelter says heating and hot water problems are often an emergency, especially in winter. If you've been left without heating for weeks, contact your council's private housing team.

Can I withhold rent if my landlord won't fix the boiler?

It's risky: rent arrears can lead to possession proceedings. Keep paying and get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice about claiming compensation instead.

Who pays for a broken boiler in a rented flat?

Your landlord, unless you or a guest damaged it. Keeping heating and hot water installations working is their legal duty.

Will my landlord pay for a heater?

There's no automatic right, but ask – and keep receipts. Extra costs caused by disrepair can form part of a compensation claim.
