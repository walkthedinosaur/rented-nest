How much notice do I need to give my landlord? (England, with template)

Usually two months, in writing, ending on a rent day or the day before. Worked date examples, a copy-and-adapt notice email, joint tenants, leaving sooner and what changed on 1 May 2026.

Last reviewed 30 October 2026 · Covers private renters in England only. This is general information, not legal advice.

The short answer: usually two months, in writing, timed so your tenancy ends on a rent day or the day before one. Your landlord can't ask for more than two months, and email or text counts as writing. You keep paying rent until the tenancy ends. If you want to leave sooner, you need your landlord to agree in writing.

Not in England? Different rules apply – see private residential tenancies in Scotland, Renting Homes in Wales and private renting in Northern Ireland.

The rules in five lines

From the GOV.UK guide for tenants and Shelter (both checked 30 October 2026):

  • Two months, at most. Your tenancy agreement should say how much notice to give. Your landlord can ask for up to two months; if the agreement says nothing, give at least two months.
  • In writing. A letter, email or text all count, and your landlord can't insist on a particular method. Keep a copy.
  • End on a rent day. The tenancy has to end on the day rent is due or the day before.
  • Pay rent to the end. You keep paying rent during the notice period.
  • Changed your mind? You can only stay if your landlord agrees in writing.

What a "periodic" tenancy means for you

GOV.UK says most existing assured shorthold tenancies automatically became assured periodic tenancies on 1 May 2026, and any new private tenancy agreed since then is one too. They run on a rolling basis – usually monthly or weekly – and an assured tenancy can no longer have an end date. If yours has one written in, it no longer applies.

In practice that means there's no fixed term to wait out: you can give notice whenever you're ready. For the bigger picture, see the Renters' Rights Act explained.

Tenancy started before 1 May 2026? GOV.UK says any valid notice you gave before 1 May still applies. Otherwise, the notice period in your agreement usually still applies – but your landlord can't require more than two months. One exception: if your fixed term ended before 1 May and your agreement didn't say what happens next, its notice period no longer applies and you use the two-month process above. GOV.UK's page on ending a tenancy that started before 1 May 2026 has the detail; Citizens Advice can help if you're unsure.

Work out your leaving date

Shelter says the notice period starts when your landlord or agent receives your notice, and it must end on the first or last day of a rental period. So count two months from the day they get it, then move forward to the next rent day (or the day before).

These are examples built from that rule, for monthly rent and two months' notice – check them against your own agreement:

  • Rent due on the 1st. Landlord receives your email on 30 November 2026. Two months later is 30 January 2027, so your tenancy can end on 31 January (the day before the 1 February rent day) – not 30 January.
  • Rent due on the 15th. Landlord receives your notice on 3 November 2026. Two months later is 3 January 2027; the next rent day is 15 January, so your last day can be 14 January 2027.
  • Rent due on the 28th. Landlord receives your notice on 25 November 2026. Two months later is 25 January 2027, so the earliest end is 27 January, the day before the 28 January rent day.

Posting a letter? Shelter suggests checking the address in your tenancy agreement, posting a few days early and getting proof of postage. If you email, ask the landlord or agent to confirm they've received it.

Copy-and-adapt notice email

Change the bits in brackets. Send it to the address for notices in your tenancy agreement (often the letting agent), and keep a copy.

Subject: Notice to end my tenancy – [full address]

Dear [landlord or agent's name],

I am giving notice to end my tenancy at [full address]. My tenancy will end on [date – a rent day or the day before], which gives at least two months' notice from the date you receive this email.

I will pay rent up to and including that date. Please could you:

  • confirm you have received this notice;
  • let me know when you'd like to arrange the check-out inspection and the return of keys;
  • confirm how my deposit will be returned. My forwarding address is [new address] and my bank details can be sent separately.

Kind regards,
[Your name(s)]
[Phone number]

Joint tenancy? Say whether you're giving notice for everyone, and talk to your housemates first (see below).

Joint tenants and house shares

If you all signed one tenancy agreement, you're joint tenants. Shelter says a valid notice from one joint tenant ends the tenancy for everyone, so talk to the people you live with before you send anything. GOV.UK adds:

  • Shorter notice needs all the other joint tenants to agree.
  • Staying on after notice has been given needs all the other joint tenants and the landlord to agree – otherwise the tenancy ends.
  • If some of you want to stay, the landlord can sign a new agreement with them.
  • Swapping one housemate for another needs everyone's and the landlord's agreement; the same terms carry on.

Each housemate on a separate agreement for their own room? Then you each give notice under your own agreement.

Leaving sooner than two months

You and your landlord can agree in writing to end the tenancy early or shorten the notice, and GOV.UK says your landlord can agree to let you leave early without paying rent for the rest of the notice period. It helps to offer something: flexible viewings, or finding a replacement tenant the landlord is happy with.

Don't just leave. Shelter warns that if you give less notice than you need, your tenancy may not end, you could still owe rent and you may have problems getting your deposit back.

FAQ

Can I give notice by text or email?

Yes. GOV.UK gives a letter, email or text as examples of notice in writing, and says your landlord can't insist on a particular method. Keep a copy, and ask them to confirm they've received it.

Can I leave my fixed term early now?

Since 1 May 2026, GOV.UK says an assured tenancy can no longer have an end date, so most private renters can give two months' notice at any time. If your tenancy started before 1 May, check GOV.UK's page on older tenancies linked above.

Does my notice have to end on the rent due date?

It has to end on the day rent is due or the day before (Shelter calls these the first and last days of a rental period). See the worked examples above.

Can I change my mind after giving notice?

Only if your landlord agrees in writing – and, in a joint tenancy, the other tenants too. Otherwise the tenancy ends on the date you gave.

Does my landlord have to give me notice too?

That's a different process with its own rules – section 21 "no fault" notices ended on 1 May 2026. See the Renters' Rights Act explained, and get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice.
