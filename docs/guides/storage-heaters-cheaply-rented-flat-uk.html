How to use storage heaters cheaply in a rented flat (incl. prepayment meters)

Storage heaters costing a fortune? Check you're on an off-peak tariff, set the input by season and the output low, skip the daytime boost – plus prepayment meter readings, what to ask your landlord and help with bills.

The short answer: storage heaters are only cheap if they charge on cheap electricity, so first check you're actually on an off-peak (time-of-use) tariff such as Economy 7. Then set the input dial by the season and leave it, keep the output dial low and turn it up only when you need heat, and avoid the boost button, which runs on peak-rate electricity. If you keep running out of heat by evening, turn the input up rather than reaching for a plug-in heater.

Below: how to check your tariff (including on a prepayment meter), the two dials explained, how to make the heat last, what to ask your landlord if the heaters are old or broken, and where to get help with bills. For cheap warmth more generally, see how to stay warm in a cold rented flat.

First check: are you on an off-peak tariff?

Storage heaters charge overnight. If your electricity costs the same around the clock, you lose the main point of having them. Citizens Advice says a time-of-use tariff is usually cheapest if you use storage heaters a lot.

  • Look at a bill. A time-of-use bill shows two or more prices per kWh ("day" and "night", or "peak" and "off-peak") and two or more sets of meter readings.
  • Look at the meter. On a standard two-rate meter, Stroud District Council's guide says the top reading (Day, High or 1) is daytime use and the lower one (Night, Low or 2) is night-time use.
  • On a prepayment meter? The same guide says to press the display button to cycle through the readings: Rate 1 is usually daytime and Rate 2 usually night-time – check with your supplier. If you only ever see one rate, ask.
  • Know your cheap hours. Citizens Advice gives Economy 7 as an example of midnight–7am from October to March and 1am–8am from March to October, but times vary by supplier, tariff and area. Ask your supplier for yours in writing.
  • Old radio-switched meter? Citizens Advice says RTS and DTS meters, which switch rates by radio signal, need replacing because the signal is being switched off – otherwise heating and hot water could stop working. Contact your supplier (and tell your landlord).

If the bills are in your name, you can usually talk to the supplier about the tariff yourself. If your landlord pays the energy bills, ask them which tariff the flat is on.

Input and output: the two dials explained

Input (sometimes "charge") sets how much heat is stored overnight. Output sets how fast the stored heat is let out during the day. The input only charges during off-peak hours, so fiddling with it during the day changes nothing.

Citizens Advice's example settings for a heater with two dials numbered 1–6 (a starting point – adjust for your flat and heater):

Input (by season)Output (by time of day, winter)
Winter: 5 or 6Overnight: lowest number
Spring and autumn: 1 to 3Morning: 3
Summer: 1Daytime: 2 · Out: 1 · Evening: 5

Stroud's guide adds: set a manual input by the season rather than resetting it every day. On an automatic input (with a thermostat), find a comfortable setting and leave it. Turn the output down or off when you go out and before bed.

Digital heater? Citizens Advice says to set a room temperature (18–21°C is usually comfortable, and not below 21°C if cold could make a health condition worse), use the timer presets such as "out all day", and switch auto boost off.

Why they run out by evening – and what not to do

The most common complaint: the flat is warm in the morning and cold by 7pm. Usually the output has been letting heat out all day, or the input is set too low for the weather.

  • Turn the input up a notch on cold nights. Stroud's guide says to do this rather than adding plug-in heaters, which cost more to run.
  • Keep the output low while you're out, then open it up in the evening.
  • Use boost only as a last resort. Boost uses peak-rate electricity. Stroud says to use it only when the stored heat has run out.
  • Check the clocks. If a heater or immersion timer shows the wrong time, it can end up heating at peak rate. The National Energy Action Economy 7 leaflet notes timers may need adjusting when the clocks change in March and October.
  • Hot water too: heat the cylinder overnight on the timer and avoid the immersion boost unless you've run out, Citizens Advice says.

Old or broken storage heaters: what to ask your landlord

Citizens Advice says that if you rent, your landlord is responsible for repairing your heating, so tell them if a storage heater isn't working. Report it in writing and keep a copy – our no heating guide has the steps and what to do if nothing happens.

  • Dials that do nothing, heaters that never warm up, or a broken timer are repair issues – describe what happens and when.
  • Check the home's EPC. In England and Wales, privately rented homes have had to meet a minimum EPC rating of E since 2020 unless an exemption applies, and the government has said it will raise this to C by 1 October 2030, subject to Parliament (GOV.UK). You can look up your home's certificate on GOV.UK's "find an energy certificate" service. A rating of F or G is worth raising with your landlord and your council.
  • Asking for newer heaters? It's fair to ask, especially if the bills show heat isn't lasting the day.

FAQ

Should I leave storage heaters on all the time?

In winter, leave them switched on at the wall so they can charge overnight; control the heat with the input and output dials. Citizens Advice suggests input 1 in summer, so many people switch them off then.

What time do storage heaters charge on Economy 7?

During your off-peak hours – often around midnight to 7am in winter, but it depends on your supplier and area. Ask your supplier.

Should I use the boost on my storage heater?

Only when the stored heat has run out. Boost runs on peak-rate electricity, which costs more.

Why are my storage heaters so expensive?

The usual causes: you're not on an off-peak tariff, the input is higher than the weather needs, boost or auto boost is on, a timer clock is wrong, or plug-in heaters are filling the gap.

Can storage heaters work on a prepayment meter?

Yes – prepayment meters can be two-rate. Press the display button to see whether you have a Rate 1 and Rate 2 reading, and check with your supplier.
