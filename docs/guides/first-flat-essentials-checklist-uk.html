Short answer: before you buy anything, check the inventory and photograph every room, take meter readings, find the stopcock and fuse box, test the smoke alarms and make sure you have your deposit protection details and safety certificates. Then buy in three rounds: day one (bedding, a few pans, cleaning kit, toilet roll), first month (storage, lighting, extra kitchen kit) and can wait (furniture and decor, once you've lived there a few weeks). Furnished flats come with more than you think, so check the inventory before you shop.

Day-one checks that protect your deposit

None of these cost anything, and they're the ones people regret skipping when the deposit comes back short.

  • Check the inventory. Your landlord or agent should give you one listing what's in the flat and its condition. Read it line by line and report anything wrong in writing. If there isn't one, Hull City Council's advice is to make your own, have someone who doesn't live there sign it, and send a copy to the landlord.
  • Photograph every room. Walls, carpets, the oven, inside cupboards, any marks or damp. Keep the dated files somewhere safe – you'll want them at move-out.
  • Take meter readings for gas, electricity and water (if metered), with photos, and send them to the suppliers.
  • Find the stopcock and fuse box so you know what to do if a pipe leaks or the power trips.
  • Test the alarms. Landlords in England must provide a smoke alarm on every storey with living space, a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed fuel-burning appliance (gas cookers excepted), and check they work at the start of the tenancy. Press the test buttons yourself and report any that don't work.
  • Check your paperwork. Your deposit should be protected in a government-approved scheme, with the prescribed information sent to you within 30 days. You should also have the gas safety record (if there's gas), the energy performance certificate and the electrical safety report. Since 1 May 2026 the Renters' Rights Act has changed what landlords must provide – check the current list on GOV.UK and see what the Renters' Rights Act changed for renters.

Kitchen essentials

Day one: one frying pan, one saucepan, a wooden spoon, a sharp knife, a chopping board, two of each plate, bowl, mug and glass, cutlery, a tea towel and washing-up liquid. First month: food tubs, a baking tray, a colander, a grater. Can wait: gadgets – see what the flat already has.

What not to buy yet:

  • Furniture before you've measured rooms, doorways and the stairs.
  • Anything already on the inventory – furnished flats often include a kettle, microwave, bed and sofa.
  • Wall-mounted anything until you've asked the landlord. Check whether Command strips are OK in a rented flat too.

FAQ

What do you need for your first flat?

For day one: bedding, a few pans and utensils, plates and mugs, cleaning kit, toilet roll and towels. Everything else can wait until you've seen what the flat comes with.

What should I check when moving into a rented flat?

The inventory, photos of every room, meter readings, where the stopcock and fuse box are, that the smoke alarms work, and that you've got your deposit protection details and safety certificates.

What kitchen things do I need for my first flat?

One frying pan, one saucepan, a knife, a chopping board, a wooden spoon, plates, bowls, mugs and cutlery. Add food tubs and a baking tray in the first month.

Can I put things on the walls in my first flat?

Check your tenancy agreement – most say no holes without permission. Ask your landlord in writing, and use freestanding or over-door storage in the meantime.
