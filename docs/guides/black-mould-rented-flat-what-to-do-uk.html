Black mould in a rented flat – what to do (England)

Mould everywhere in your rented flat? What to do this week: photograph it, clean a small patch safely, report it in writing, how to make your landlord act, and whether they can evict you for complaining.

The short answer: photograph the mould before you touch it, clean small patches gently with soapy water while wearing gloves, then report it to your landlord or letting agent in writing and keep copies of everything. Finding and fixing the cause is usually your landlord's job. If they ignore you, your council's environmental health team can inspect and can order them to fix it. And since 1 May 2026 a private landlord in England can't use a "no fault" section 21 notice – they need a legal reason and a court hearing to evict you.

Before you clean: photograph and record it

Once you've wiped it away, the evidence has gone. Shelter says you need evidence of problems if you later want compensation, and that you should keep a record of all conversations, emails and messages. Before cleaning:

  • Take dated photos of every patch, with something in the shot for scale, plus a wider photo showing where it is in the room.
  • Note what's damaged – clothes, shoes, a mattress, furniture – and keep receipts if you have to replace anything.
  • Log the humidity. A cheap hygrometer gives you dated readings that show the problem continues even when you're ventilating.
  • Keep a simple diary: when it came back, what you've done (opening windows, running fans), and every contact with your landlord or agent.

How to clean a small patch of mould safely

Cleaning doesn't fix the cause, but it stops a small patch spreading while the cause is investigated. Advice from Enfield Council on cleaning small areas of mould:

  1. Protect yourself: goggles, long rubber gloves and a mask that covers your nose and mouth.
  2. Clear the room of other people and pets. Open the window but keep internal doors shut so spores don't spread.
  3. Bag up mouldy soft things – wash clothes and soft toys at a high temperature; shampoo or dry-clean soft furnishings.
  4. Wipe gently with a cloth dipped in water and mild detergent such as washing-up liquid. The council says specialist mould cleaners are an option but hot soapy water works just as well. Don't brush mould – that can release spores.
  5. Dry the area with a dry cloth, bag up and bin the cloths, and wipe the other hard surfaces in the room.

Don't paint over it. Shelter says a landlord shouldn't tell you to paint over mould that keeps coming back. If it covers a large area, keeps returning, or someone in the home has health problems, leave the cleaning to your landlord's contractor and report it straight away.

Report it to your landlord in writing

Tell your landlord or letting agent in writing, even if you've already mentioned it in person. Email or text is fine if you keep the messages; Shelter has a template letter for repairs. Say:

  • which rooms are affected and when you first noticed it;
  • what you've done to keep moisture down;
  • what's been damaged, and any effect on anyone's health;
  • when you can give access for an inspection.

Attach your photos and readings. Shelter says your landlord must try to find the cause and, if they can't, should get a damp surveyor – and you don't have to pay for this. It also says they should act urgently if your health is at risk, and check whether the damp returns at least 6 weeks after repairs.

While you wait, keep moisture down so things don't get worse: lids on pans, washing on an airer in one room with the window open rather than on radiators, and the bathroom door shut while you shower. If your landlord ignores it: how to make them act

  • Chase in writing and keep copies of every reply, call and visit.
  • Complain about the agent if a letting agent ignores you or won't pass the problem on – Shelter says you can.
  • Contact your council. If the problem is serious, you can ask the council for an environmental health inspection, and the council can order your landlord to fix it (Shelter). Councils may call this team "private sector housing".
  • Think about compensation later. Shelter says you may be able to claim if damp damages your belongings, harms your health, makes your home unfit to live in, or costs you money – for example buying a dehumidifier. You usually have at least 3 years to start a claim, so it can wait until after you move out. Keep the receipts.
  • Don't just move out. Shelter's advice is not to move out unless you have somewhere to go: if you don't end the tenancy properly your landlord could keep your deposit or claim rent, and a council could say you've made yourself homeless. Get advice first.

Awaab's Law – fixed deadlines for investigating and fixing damp and mould – already applies to social housing. Extending it to private renting is out for consultation until 18 December 2026, so it isn't law for private landlords yet. See what's changing.

Can my landlord evict me for complaining about mould?

It's a common worry, and it stops people reporting mould. Here's where things stand in England:

  • No more "no fault" evictions. GOV.UK: from 1 May 2026 you can no longer be evicted using the section 21 process. A landlord now has to use a section 8 notice and rely on a legal reason (a "ground"), such as selling or serious rent arrears, usually with four months' notice. Shelter says there will always be a court hearing.
  • The landlord has to prove a ground. Grounds are specific reasons set out in law – the examples Colchester Council gives tenants are selling the property, serious rent arrears and anti-social behaviour. Shelter points out that a private landlord can't evict you without giving a section 8 notice, so keep paying rent and keep your side of the tenancy while the repair is sorted.
  • Had a section 21 notice before 1 May? Older notices have their own rules – see our Renters' Rights Act guide and get advice quickly.

If you're threatened with eviction, or harassed after complaining, contact Shelter, Citizens Advice or your council for advice on your own situation. Rules are different in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

FAQ

Is black mould the landlord's responsibility?

Usually the cause is. You're expected to do everyday things to keep moisture down, but if mould keeps coming back the cause is usually something about the home – ventilation, heating, insulation or a defect – and that's your landlord's job to find and fix. More in is condensation the landlord's responsibility?

How do I make my landlord fix mould?

Report it in writing with photos, chase in writing, and if nothing happens or it's serious, ask your council for an environmental health inspection – the council can order your landlord to fix it.

Can I withhold rent because of mould?

Don't stop paying rent without advice – it can put your home at risk, because serious rent arrears are a ground for eviction. Talk to Shelter or Citizens Advice about your options first.

Can I claim compensation for mould?

Possibly, if mould has damaged your belongings, affected your health or cost you money. Shelter says you usually have at least 3 years to start a claim, and you'll need evidence – so keep photos, receipts and your damp log.
