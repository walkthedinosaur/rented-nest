Heated clothes airers for small flats (UK)

No garden, no tumble dryer? How to choose a heated clothes airer for a small UK flat – types, running costs, safety and avoiding damp.

In a rented flat with no garden and no tumble dryer, winter washing ends up on every radiator and chair back. A heated airer is the usual middle ground: it plugs into a normal socket, warms its rails gently and folds away afterwards. Which? found the best models cost as little as 4p an hour to run, and the most costly around 29p. Here's how to pick one that suits a small space, and how to use it without inviting damp.

The main types of heated airer

Winged heated airer

A flat central section with fold-down wings either side – the classic shape. They're the lowest-powered type (Which? puts them at around 230W) and the cheapest to buy, but they take up a fair bit of floor when the wings are up. A good fit for a living room you can clear for an evening. Typically around £50–£60 at the time of writing – check current price.

How to choose for a small flat

Before you buy, measure the spot where it will stand and where it will live when folded. Then check:

  • Drying space – listed in metres of rail. More is useful only if you can space clothes out; crammed rails dry slowly.
  • Maximum load – usually given in kg. Stay under it, especially with wet towels and jeans.
  • Folded size – the depth matters most if it's going behind a door or down the side of a wardrobe.
  • Stability – wide feet or locking castors, especially with pets or children around.
  • Cord length – Which? lists cords from about 132cm to 180cm. Pick a spot near a wall socket rather than relying on an extension lead.

Making it work in a rented flat

Every load of washing releases water into the air, and in a small flat that moisture ends up on cold windows and walls. Condensation and mould are common sources of end-of-tenancy disputes, so it's worth getting the routine right.

  • Spin hard first – the drier clothes are going on, the less water goes into the room.
  • Space items out and turn them – Which? notes that undisturbed laundry dries much more slowly. Turn thick items halfway through.
  • Use the cover – it keeps warmth on the clothes rather than heating the room.
  • Ventilate – dry in a room with the door shut and a window cracked open or an extractor fan on, not in a bedroom you sleep in.

A dehumidifier alongside the airer

A dehumidifier pulls moisture out of the air while the washing dries, and some have a dedicated laundry mode. It adds to the electricity bill, but in a damp flat it helps protect walls, windows and your deposit.

Staying safe

Heated airers run at low temperatures, but they're still electrical heaters. Which? passes on the advice most brands give:

  • Don't leave it running overnight or while you're out.
  • Don't overload it, especially with thick, heavy items.
  • Keep it away from curtains and soft furnishings.
  • Plug it straight into a wall socket and avoid cheap extension leads.

Read the maker's instructions too – they set out what the airer is and isn't designed for.
