Hi,

Thanks for confirming. Here's your checklist:

**[Download the Winter Damp & Warmth Checklist (PDF)](https://therentednest.co.uk/checklists/winter-damp-warmth-checklist.pdf)**

It's one page, written for renters. Nothing on it needs drilling or your landlord's permission, except the items
that say "ask first".

If you only do three things this week:
1. **Wipe the windows every morning.** Condensation that's left on glass and sills soaks into frames and feeds mould.
2. **Dry washing in one room with the door shut** and a window cracked or a fan on. Avoid the bedroom you sleep in.
3. **Write down anything that's your landlord's job.** That means a broken extractor fan, a leak, or mould that keeps
   coming back. Report it by email and keep a copy.

Not sure where the line is between your job and theirs? This guide covers it:
[Is condensation the landlord's responsibility?](https://therentednest.co.uk/guides/condensation-landlord-responsibility-uk.html)

## Email 2 – damp & warmth tips + the calculator (send: +2 days)
**Subject:** Heated airer, dryer or dehumidifier – which costs least to run?
**Preview text:** A free calculator, plus renter-friendly ways to stop draughts.

Hi,

The most common winter question we see from renters is how to get washing dry without paying a fortune and without
the flat going mouldy.

**1. Work out the running cost before you buy.**
Our free [drying & heating running-cost calculator](https://therentednest.co.uk/tools/drying-heating-cost-calculator.html)
compares a heated airer, tumble dryer, dehumidifier and plug-in heater. It shows the cost per use, per week, per
month and per winter at the current UK price cap rate. Put in your own wattage and hours, and it does the sums.

**2. Get washing dry without a balcony.**
A simple routine helps: an extra spin, one room with the door shut, the airer kept off the radiator, then ventilate or
run a dehumidifier. Read it here: [How to dry clothes in a flat without a balcony](https://therentednest.co.uk/guides/dry-clothes-in-a-flat-without-a-balcony-uk.html).

**3. Stop the draughts without losing your deposit.**
Door excluders, thermal curtains and removable window film all keep heat in and come off cleanly. Never block
the vents. See: [How to draught-proof a rented flat without damaging it](https://therentednest.co.uk/guides/draught-proofing-a-rented-flat-uk.html).

**4. If you're thinking about a dehumidifier,**
our [dehumidifier guide for rented flats](https://therentednest.co.uk/guides/dehumidifiers-for-rented-flats-uk.html)
explains which size and type (compressor or desiccant) suits a small flat. It also covers what is still your
landlord's job to fix.

One more thing: if damp or mould keeps coming back even though you ventilate, that usually points to the building,
not to how you live in it. Report it in writing.
