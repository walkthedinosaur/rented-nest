## 1. Housing association says we owe £2,750 – "We have never missed a rent payment"
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6690541/housing-association-pinnacle-claiming-2-750-rent-arrears (13 Nov,
open). Tenants since Jan 2025; a "First Reminder" (12 Nov) claims £2,749.78; the statement starts at £0 in March 2025 and
leaves out two card payments from Jan/Feb 2025; rent is charged on the 1st but the direct debit clears around the 19th.
> Draft: Two separate things may be going on. First, timing: if your agreement says rent is due on the 1st and your
> direct debit goes out on the 19th, the account will show you "in arrears" for most of every month even though you've
> never missed a payment. Ask them in writing to either move the debit to the due date or confirm it's an agreed
> arrangement and not real arrears. Second, the missing payments: ask for a full rent statement from the very start of
> the tenancy (not from March 2025) and put your bank records next to it line by line – list each payment with date and
> amount in one email so they can't miss it. Ask them to put any recovery action on hold while they check. If they still
> won't sort it, use their formal complaints procedure; housing association tenants can take an unresolved complaint to
> the Housing Ombudsman. Citizens Advice can go through the statement with you.

## 2. Can we register on the council's housing list on a £65k income?
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6690573/gateway-to-home-choice-eligibility-question (13 Nov, open).
Private renters in a 3-storey house; an OT recommended a bungalow for a partner with mobility issues; worried how
credit use and a little online gambling on bank statements will look.
> Draft: Every council writes its own allocation policy, so the answer is in your council's policy, not a national rule –
> ask the housing team in writing what income or savings limit applies and whether medical/disability need changes it.
> Send the OT's report with your application; that's the evidence that usually matters for a move on medical grounds.
> Be straightforward with the bank statements rather than delaying them – a few months of tight budgeting isn't unusual,
> and anything that looks hidden tends to cause more questions than the spending itself. While you wait (lists can be
> long), it's worth asking the OT whether adaptations to where you live now, or a ground-floor private let, could help
> sooner.

## 3. Landlord asked us to suggest a fair rent after 11 years at the same level
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6686386/rent-increase-after-11-years-at-same-level (Oct, active to 10 Nov).
3-bed house since 2011, no rises, tenants do small repairs; landlord abroad, may sell; he floated £400–£700/month more;
tenants could manage about £300. Renter: "NOT moving house is our ideal at the moment."
> Draft: Look up what similar homes nearby are actually advertised for (same size, same condition – not freshly done-up
> ones), so your offer is anchored on evidence rather than a percentage a year. Then put your offer in writing and list
> what you've been doing: the repairs and upkeep you've covered for 11 years have a real value, and receipts or photos
> make that concrete. Offering a sensible rise now in exchange for the current arrangement carrying on is often better
> for both sides than a big jump, a vacant house and the cost of re-letting. For reference, since 1 May 2026 rent in
> England can normally only go up once a year with at least two months' notice on the proper form, and if a formal
> increase is above the open market rent you can ask the First-tier Tribunal to decide – Shelter explains the steps.

## 4. 30% rent increase after eight years – "We cannot absorb a 30% increase"
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6686202/30-rent-increase (Oct, active to 28 Oct; no outcome posted).
Landlord rejected a counter-offer and gave two months; tenant has surgery coming up.
> Draft: First check the paperwork: since 1 May 2026 a rent rise in England has to come on the proper notice (a section 13
> notice) with at least two months' notice, and only once a year. If something's wrong with it – wrong form, too little
> notice, wrong start date – tell the landlord in writing. If the notice is valid but you think 30% is more than the home
> would let for now, you can apply to the First-tier Tribunal to set the rent, **but you must apply before the new rent's
> start date**, so don't wait. Gather 3–5 adverts for genuinely similar homes nearby. Shelter's helpline and Citizens
> Advice can check the notice with you, and with surgery coming up it's worth asking the landlord in writing for a later
> start date while this is sorted.

## 5. Agent wants £194 for a professional clean after eight years – "Surely this is fair wear and tear"
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6687819/challenging-a-cleaning-fee-request-after-vacating-a-flat (23 Oct,
open – deposit scheme dispute pending). Items: dust in an extractor vent, mould in bath sealant, oven glass.
> Draft: In the deposit scheme's dispute the landlord/agent has to show the flat was left in worse condition than at the
> start, allowing for fair wear and tear and for how long you lived there – eight years is a long time for sealant and an
> oven door. Ask for the check-in inventory and the itemised cleaning invoice if you don't have them, and send the
> scheme your own photos from move-out plus anything showing what you cleaned. Answer each item separately (vent: a
> quick wipe, not a professional clean; sealant mould: ageing; oven glass: say what you did). Schemes can award part of
> a claim, so a fair offer of a small amount for anything you accept can be worth making.

## 6. 13 years in a rented farmhouse: leaks, damp, "advance payment", now told to look elsewhere
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6613116/rights-as-a-tenant (June 2025 – older; page 1 shows no outcome).
£2,155 paid as "an advance payment", not protected; leaking roof, bad windows, damp and mould; landlord now says find
somewhere else. **Note: the law changed after this thread started.**
> Draft: If you're still there: since 1 May 2026 landlords in England can no longer use a "no fault" section 21 notice, so
> "start looking" isn't by itself a way to end your tenancy – a landlord now needs a legal ground and the proper notice.
> Check what that £2,155 really was: if it works like a deposit, it should have been protected in one of the three
> government-backed schemes, and if it wasn't, that matters – Shelter explains what you can claim. For the leaks and
> mould, report every problem in writing with dated photos; if nothing is done, the council's private housing team can
> inspect. With a child's health involved, mention that in your written reports. Shelter or Citizens Advice can look at
> the whole picture before you decide whether to push for repairs or plan a move on your terms.

## 7. Working abroad up to 16 weeks a year – can I keep my room in an HMO?
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6683921/how-do-you-stay-as-a-tennant-when-you-are-overseas (19 Sep, page 1
no outcome). Renter: "Do you think my landlord is going to be OK for me".
> Draft: Ask the landlord now, in writing, before your first trip – most landlords are happy to keep a tenant who keeps
> paying, but some insurance policies have rules about rooms being empty for long periods, so they'll want to know
> dates. Give them the expected trip lengths, a contact while you're away and someone local who can get in if needed.
> Tell your own contents insurer too, and set rent up so it can't lapse while you're abroad. Get the reply in writing.
