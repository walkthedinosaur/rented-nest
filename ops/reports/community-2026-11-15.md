### 1. "Housing association claiming £2,750 rent arrears" – but "never missed a rent payment" (13 Nov 2026, England)
Thread: forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6690541 · renter words: "ghost balance", "Mid-Month Billing Trap",
"full ledger reconciliation"
> Draft: Line up every charge and every payment month by month from the day you moved in – a simple table with "rent
> due", "paid", "running balance". Posters already spotted one month charged twice, so look for duplicates first. Check
> your tenancy agreement for the rent due date: if rent is due on the 1st but your Direct Debit goes out later, the
> account can show "arrears" for part of every month even though you pay in full. Send the table to the landlord in
> writing and ask them to correct the statement and pause any recovery letters while it's checked. Keep the Direct
> Debit running so no real arrears build up. If they don't fix it, use their formal complaints process – Shelter's
> free helpline can help you word it.

### 2. Giving two months' notice on a rental while buying – how to avoid paying rent and mortgage together (4 Nov 2026)
Thread: /discussion/6689356 · renter words: "crossover period of paying rent and the mortgage"
> Draft: There's no fixed gap between exchange and completion – it's agreed between everyone in the chain, so ask your
> solicitor (not the estate agent) what's realistic. Most people budget for a few weeks of overlap; it's also handy
> for cleaning and getting the deposit back. Under the new rules in England your notice has to end at the end of a
> rental period, so check your rent date before you serve it – a few days either way can add almost a month. You can
> also ask your landlord in writing whether they'd agree to a shorter notice period once you have a completion date.

### 3. "How often is too often for an inspection when renting?" (5 Nov 2026 – asked by a landlord, 3 pages)
Thread: /discussion/6689417 · the renter version of this question: "my landlord wants to inspect every 3 months"
> Draft (renter angle): Most replies – including landlords – said every 6–12 months is normal once a tenancy has
> settled, and quarterly is a lot. Your landlord needs to give you written notice (at least 24 hours) and visit at a
> reasonable time, and you can ask to move a date that doesn't suit. A short, friendly reply proposing a different
> time usually works better than refusing outright. Report repairs as they come up – that's what most landlords really
> want from inspections.
> *(Fact check before use: our landlord-entry guide's notice wording.)*

### 4. "Landlord made £450 claim for deposit and threatens £1500 plus if I don't settle" (Jan 2026)
Thread: /discussion/6650499 · renter words: "damaged beyond repair", "fight it on principle or settle"
> Draft: Go through the claim item by item. The landlord has to show you caused damage beyond fair wear and tear, using
> the check-in and check-out reports – a stain doesn't automatically mean a whole new floor, and they can't charge you
> for brand-new replacements of old items. Keep the cleaner's written confirmation and any messages where the landlord
> told you not to fix something. If the deposit is protected, the scheme's free dispute service decides only on the
> deposit money; a bigger claim would have to go through the courts, so keep calm about threats and keep everything
> in writing. Offering to settle the one or two items that are fair can make the rest easier to win.

### 5. "Can my student daughter now give 2 months notice and end her tenancy?" (26 Apr 2026, 6-person student house)
Thread: /discussion/6665734 · renter words: "finish uni by 1st July" but "pay until 31st August"
> Draft: Since 1 May 2026 most private tenancies in England are rolling, and tenants can usually leave with two months'
> written notice ending at the end of a rent period. In a joint tenancy for six, how one person's notice affects the
> others matters a lot – talk to the housemates and check with Shelter or the uni's housing advice service before
> anyone serves notice. Purpose-built student halls can be different. Send notice in writing, to the address or email
> the tenancy says to use, and keep proof.

### 6. Tenancy change after bereavement: new contract says "no pets" and "5% minimum" rent rises (Feb 2026)
Thread: /discussion/6656882 · renter words: "I'm not in a position to refuse to sign"
> Draft: I'm sorry for your loss. You don't have to sign a new contract to stay – if you were a joint tenant, the
> tenancy may simply continue in your name. Under the new rules you can ask to keep a pet and the landlord can't
> unreasonably refuse; a cat that has lived there for 10 years is a strong case. Rent rises now have to follow the
> formal process too. Before replying to the agent, ring Shelter or Citizens Advice to check your position, then reply
> politely in writing.

### 7. "Will it be impossible without 'income' to get a place to rent?" – savings but no payslip (Dec 2025)
Thread: /discussion/6644885 · renter words: "show income", "rent in advance"
> Draft: It's the letting agent's referencing, not the law, that usually asks for a payslip. Ask agents up front
> whether they accept proof of savings or investment income, or a guarantor. Big rent-in-advance offers are mostly off
> the table now – the new rules in England cap upfront rent at around one month – so proof of funds and a good
> reference from your current landlord matter more. Private landlords who let directly are often more flexible.
> *(Fact check before use: the exact upfront-rent rule on GOV.UK.)*

### 8. 13 years renting: leaking roof, damp and mould, deposit called an "advance payment" (Jun 2025)
Thread: /discussion/6613116 · renter words: "Never missed a payment", "what are our rights and what should our next move be?"
> Draft: Start a dated record now: photos of the mould and leaks, copies of every message reporting them. Report the
> repairs in writing. If nothing happens, your council's private housing team can inspect. Call the "advance payment"
> what it is: if it was meant as a deposit, it should have been protected in one of the three schemes – check all
> three online. A landlord can't just tell you to leave; there's a legal process. Shelter's helpline can go through
> your options, including what to do about the pets.

### 9. Rent went "from 700 to 900", then the landlord wants "a months notice" when I leave (Jul 2025)
Thread: /discussion/6616964 · renter words: "a random CCJ showing up against me"
> Draft: Your tenancy agreement (and now the new rules) decide the notice period, not earlier chats. If you owe a
> month's rent for the notice period, paying it – or agreeing a shorter period in writing – is the safest way to
> avoid a county court claim. If you think the rent rise wasn't done properly, check with your council whether the
> property needed a licence, and with Shelter about challenging the rise.

### 10. Mould in the upstairs bedroom of a watertight house (Dec 2025 – asked by a landlord, 5 pages)
Thread: /discussion/6645666 · renter version: "we've got a 12L dehumidifier but the bedroom still goes mouldy"
> Draft (renter angle): Replies agreed: run a dehumidifier in the problem room for long stretches (many said at least
> 12 hours a day), empty it daily and keep the door shut while it runs. Use the extractor fans every time you cook or
> shower and leave them running after. Keep furniture a few centimetres off cold outside walls. If the mould keeps
> coming back, report it in writing – ventilation and insulation problems are the landlord's to fix.
