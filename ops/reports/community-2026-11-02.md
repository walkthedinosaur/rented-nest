### 1. My UK landlord left me without a gas boiler, no hot water and no heating in the house for 6 and half weeks, what sort of rent reduction can I expect and can I withhold rent until the matter is clarified? *(3 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/My-UK-landlord-left-me-without-a-gas-boiler-no-hot-water-and-no-heating-in-the-house-for-6-and-half-weeks-what-sort-of-rent-reduction-can-I-expect-and-can-I-withhold-rent-until-the-matter-is-clarified

**Draft answer:** Don't simply stop paying rent – in England that can put you in arrears, and arrears give a landlord
grounds to try to end the tenancy, even when they're the one in the wrong. Keeping the heating and hot water working
is the landlord's legal responsibility (it's one of the repairs they can't contract out of), and they have to fix it
within a reasonable time once they know about it. Six weeks without heating or hot water is very unlikely to count as
reasonable. What to do: report it in writing (email is fine) and keep a dated record of every contact, what you've
had to spend (heaters, extra electricity, showers elsewhere) and photos. If nothing happens, contact your council's
private housing or environmental health team – lack of heating can be a hazard they can act on. Compensation (a rent
refund or damages) is something you claim, not something you deduct yourself; Shelter or Citizens Advice can tell you
how strong your case is and how to ask for it. (General information, not legal advice. Scotland, Wales and NI differ.)

### 2. Are landlords and tenants in the UK required to have annual boiler checks? If so, what are the legal requirements? *(1 answer)*
https://www.quora.com/Are-landlords-and-tenants-in-the-UK-required-to-have-annual-boiler-checks-If-so-what-are-the-legal-requirements

**Draft answer:** Landlords, yes; tenants, no. If a rented home has gas appliances (boiler, hob, fire), the landlord
must have them safety-checked every year by a Gas Safe registered engineer and give the tenant a copy of the gas
safety record – new tenants before they move in, existing tenants within 28 days of the check. The landlord also has
to keep the appliances and pipework in safe condition between checks. Tenants have to give reasonable access for the
check (with notice) and should report problems such as a yellow flame, soot or a carbon monoxide alarm going off
straight away. If you've never been given a gas safety record, ask for it in writing. If you smell gas, call the
National Gas Emergency number and leave – don't wait for the landlord. (General information, not legal advice.)

### 3. What is the possibility that my UK landlord will pay to switch from electric to gas heating? I'm aware it's not a legal requirement but electric heating is too expensive for me to run… *(7 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-possibility-that-my-UK-landlord-will-pay-to-switch-from-electric-to-gas-heating-I-m-aware-it-s-not-a-legal-requirement-but-electric-heating-is-too-expensive-for-me-to-run-Could-I-be-expected-to

**Draft answer:** You're right that there's no requirement to switch, and a switch to gas is a big job (a gas supply,
boiler, pipes and radiators), so most landlords will say no – especially as gas boilers are being phased down in new
homes. Things that are more likely to work: check the flat's Energy Performance Certificate on the government's EPC
register – private rentals in England and Wales generally need at least an E rating, and the report lists cheaper
improvements (insulation, better heater controls) you can point to. Ask the landlord in writing for those specific
fixes rather than a whole new system. On your side, check your tariff: if you have storage heaters you need an
off-peak tariff (such as Economy 7) for them to make sense, and heating the person (heated throw, electric blanket,
layers) costs far less than heating every room. (General information, not legal advice.)

### 4. I recently moved into a flat in the UK that is extremely cold. The heating has to be constantly on at full blast in order for it to be a little warm. Once heating is turned off it goes back to freezing within 15–20 minutes… *(13 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/I-recently-moved-into-a-flat-in-the-UK-that-is-extremely-cold-The-heating-has-to-be-constantly-on-at-full-blast-in-order-for-it-to-be-a-little-warm-Once-heating-is-turned-off-it-goes-back-to-freezing-within-15-20

**Draft answer:** A flat that loses heat that fast usually has a draught problem, poor insulation, or both. First, look
up its EPC on the government register – it shows the rating and what's insulated. Then find the draughts: hold a
damp hand or a lit incense stick (carefully) near window frames, the front door, the letterbox, floorboards and
loft hatches. Many fixes need no drilling and come off cleanly when you move out – brush or foam strips on doors,
a letterbox cover, a door curtain on a tension rod, thermal curtains, a rug over gappy boards. Anything broken or
badly fitted (gaps round frames, rotten windows, a heating system that can't heat the space) is worth reporting to
your landlord in writing, because excess cold can be a hazard the council can act on. In the meantime, keep the
heating low and steady rather than blasting it and switching off, and close doors to rooms you aren't using.
(General information, not legal advice.)

### 5. How do I heat my home cheaply? I moved into a flat, all electric with storage heaters and a prepayment meter. It's costing me £5 or more a day just to run the heating… *(19 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-heat-my-home-cheaply-I-moved-into-a-flat-all-electric-with-storage-heaters-and-a-prepayment-meter-Its-costing-me-5-or-more-a-day-just-to-run-the-heating-Its-cold-in-the-UK-now-and-prices-are-going-up-Should

**Draft answer:** Storage heaters only make sense on an off-peak tariff (Economy 7 or similar), where they charge up
overnight on cheaper electricity. First, ask your supplier exactly which tariff and meter you're on – if you're paying
the same rate day and night, you're paying peak prices for night-time charging, and that alone can explain the bills.
Then learn the two dials: **input** sets how much heat is stored overnight (lower it on milder days), **output** (or
"boost") controls how fast it's released – keep it low in the morning so heat lasts into the evening. Avoid using any
"boost" or convector function in the daytime, which runs on peak-rate electricity. Close curtains at dusk, block
draughts, and heat yourself rather than the whole flat. If you're struggling, ask your supplier about the Warm Home
Discount and their hardship support, and ask your landlord whether the heaters are old manual models that could be
upgraded. (General information.)

### 6. We live in a Victorian house converted into 4 flats. One of the neighbours insists that the windows in the communal area have to be open 24/7. Landlord backed her up. Will it cause damp problems? *(5 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/We-live-in-a-Victorian-house-converted-into-4-flats-One-of-the-neighbours-insists-that-the-windows-in-communal-area-have-to-be-open-24-7-Landlord-backed-her-up-Will-it-cause-damp-problems-How-can-I-justify-shutting

**Draft answer:** An open window in a communal hallway is more likely to reduce damp than cause it – airflow lets
moist air escape. The real cost is warmth: a cold hallway pulls heat out of the flats next to it, especially through
gappy front doors. A fair compromise to suggest to the landlord: windows open on a small setting or for set periods
(after cleaning, in the mornings) rather than wide open all day and night. Inside your own flat, a draught strip on
your front door and a door curtain keep the cold hallway out without touching the communal area. If there's an actual
damp or mould problem in the hall, that's the landlord's to fix – report it in writing. (General information.)

### 7. How do people in small UK flats manage with limited space for drying clothes, especially when avoiding dryer cycles? *(3 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/How-do-people-in-small-UK-flats-manage-with-limited-space-for-drying-clothes-especially-when-avoiding-dryer-cycles

**Draft answer:** Most people use a mix of: a folding or tiered airer in the room with the best airflow (often the
bathroom with the fan on, or a bedroom with the door shut and the window on the latch); a heated airer, which uses a
fraction of a tumble dryer's power and dries a load overnight; and a dehumidifier next to the airer, which speeds
drying a lot and stops the moisture ending up as condensation on your windows. Two habits make the biggest difference:
spin everything on the fastest spin your washing machine allows, and don't hang wet washing on radiators – it adds
moisture to the room and slows the radiator heating the space. Do smaller, more frequent loads so the airer isn't
overloaded. (General information.)

### 8. How can I dry my clothes if I live in a small flat on the ground floor with no sun? *(2 answers)*
https://www.quora.com/How-can-I-dry-my-clothes-if-I-live-in-a-small-flat-on-the-ground-floor-with-no-sun

**Draft answer:** You don't need sun – you need moving air and somewhere for the moisture to go. Put the airer in one
room with the door shut, open the window a little or run the extractor fan, and if you can, put a dehumidifier in the
same room: it pulls water out of the air (and the clothes) much faster than leaving them to hang. A heated airer helps
for thick items like towels and jeans; a cover over it traps the warmth. Ground-floor flats can be prone to damp, so
watch for condensation on windows in the morning – if it's streaming, that's a sign the room needs more ventilation
while you dry washing. (General information.)

### 9. Who is responsible for painting – a landlord or tenant in the UK? *(1 answer)*
https://www.quora.com/Who-is-responsible-for-painting-a-landlord-or-tenant-in-the-UK

**Draft answer:** Routine redecoration is generally the landlord's cost – paint wears with time, and normal wear and
tear isn't something a tenant pays for. A tenant is responsible for damage they cause beyond that: big marks, holes
from fixings, or painting a room a colour without permission. Check your tenancy agreement, which may say you can't
redecorate without written consent; if you want to paint, get that consent in writing first, including whether the
landlord expects it painted back at the end. (General information, not legal advice. England.)

### 10. Can a landlord charge you for painting after you move out in the UK? *(1 answer)*
https://www.quora.com/Can-a-landlord-charge-you-for-painting-after-you-move-out-in-the-UK

**Draft answer:** Only for damage you caused, and only a fair share. A landlord can't charge you to repaint walls
just because the paint has aged – that's fair wear and tear – and they shouldn't charge the full cost of a full
redecoration when only one wall was marked. If your deposit is in a government-backed protection scheme (it should
be), you can dispute any deduction you disagree with through the scheme's free dispute service; the landlord has to
show evidence, which is where the check-in inventory and photos matter. Your own move-in and move-out photos are your
best protection. (General information, not legal advice. England.)
