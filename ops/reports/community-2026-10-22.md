### 1. Is a landlord legally required to deal with mould in a tenant's house?
https://www.quora.com/Is-a-landlord-legally-required-to-deal-with-mould-in-a-tenant-s-house

**Draft answer:** In England, generally yes. A rented home has to be fit to live in. The government's guidance for
tenants lists damp and mould growth among the hazards that can make a home unfit. Government guidance for landlords
says they're legally responsible for dealing with damp and mould and should fix the cause, not just wipe the
surface. Tenants are expected to do the everyday things: ventilate, use extractor fans, don't dry washing on
radiators. If mould keeps coming back anyway, that usually points to the building, such as ventilation, insulation,
heating or a leak. Report it in writing, keep dated photos, and if nothing happens, contact your council's
environmental health team. Shelter and Citizens Advice can advise on your own case. (General information, not legal
advice. Rules differ in Scotland, Wales and NI.)

### 2. There is mould everywhere in my flat and my landlord, who knew before I moved in that there is a problem with mould, isn't really helpful… What can I do?
https://www.quora.com/There-is-mould-everywhere-in-my-flat-and-my-landlord-who-knew-before-I-moved-in-that-there-is-a-problem-with-mould-isnt-really-helpful-What-can-I-do

**Draft answer:** Get it on record first. Email your landlord or agent describing where the mould is and how long
it's been there, and attach dated photos. If they knew about the problem before you moved in, say so; it helps show
it isn't down to how you live. Ask them to investigate the cause and give a date. Meanwhile, keep doing the basics
(ventilate, wipe windows, keep furniture off cold walls) and note that you're doing them. A cheap humidity monitor
gives you dated readings. If they don't act, your council's environmental health team can inspect for free and can
order the landlord to fix it. Don't stop paying rent and don't move out without getting advice from Shelter or
Citizens Advice first. (England; general information, not legal advice.)

### 3. What can I do about my landlord evicting me because I complained about the mould?
https://www.quora.com/What-can-I-do-about-my-landlord-evicting-me-because-I-complained-about-the-mould

**Draft answer:** If you rent privately in England, the rules changed on 1 May 2026. Landlords can no longer use
"no fault" section 21 evictions. To end a tenancy they have to rely on a specific legal ground, use the right forms,
usually give four months' notice, and Shelter says there will always be a court hearing. Complaining about a repair
isn't a ground. If you've been given any notice, check exactly which form it is and the date you got it, keep
reporting the mould in writing, and get advice from Shelter or Citizens Advice straight away. Don't move out before
you've spoken to someone. (England only; general information, not legal advice.)

### 4. Can I make my landlord get rid of the dampness and mould on my walls?
https://www.quora.com/Can-I-make-my-landlord-get-rid-of-the-dampness-and-mould-on-my-walls

**Draft answer:** You can't force it overnight, but there's a clear route. (1) Report it in writing and keep a copy.
Email or text is fine. (2) Gather evidence: dated photos, humidity readings, damaged belongings. (3) Let them in to
inspect at an agreed time and give them a reasonable time to act. (4) Chase in writing. (5) If nothing happens or
it's serious, contact your council's environmental health team. They can inspect and order repairs. Shelter says
the landlord should find the cause, should use a damp surveyor if they can't, and you shouldn't pay for that.
Painting over mould that keeps coming back isn't a fix. (England; general information, not legal advice.)

### 5. Can a tenant claim back rent if there is black mould in their flat caused by the landlord's failure to have heating on during poor weather conditions, causing condensation?
https://www.quora.com/search?q=condensation%20rented%20flat (found via this search; the question link wasn't captured)

**Draft answer:** Possibly, but it usually works as a claim for compensation (damaged belongings, health effects,
loss of use of a room), not an automatic rent refund. Shelter says you have at least three years to start a claim,
and claims need evidence: dated photos, repair requests and replies, receipts, and any medical notes. Don't just stop
paying rent. That can put you in arrears, which is a ground for eviction. If the heating is broken or not working
properly, report it as a repair in writing; that's the landlord's job. For a claim, talk to Shelter or Citizens
Advice first. (England; general information, not legal advice.)

### 6. How much notice should a landlord give for a rent increase in the United Kingdom (UK)? *(Quora: unanswered)*
https://www.quora.com/unanswered/How-much-notice-should-a-landlord-give-for-a-rent-increase-in-the-United-Kingdom-UK

**Draft answer:** For private renters in England, since 1 May 2026 rent can only go up once a year, and not in the
first 12 months of a new tenancy. The landlord has to use the official form (Form 4A) and give at least two months'
notice. If you think the new rent is above market rent, you can challenge it at a tribunal. Get advice as soon as the
notice arrives. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own rules, so check the government guidance for where
you live. (General information, not legal advice.)

### 7. Do landlords increase the rent every year in the UK? *(Quora: unanswered)*
https://www.quora.com/unanswered/Do-landlords-increase-the-rent-every-year-in-the-UK

**Draft answer:** Not automatically. Many do, but they don't have to. For private tenants in England, since 1 May
2026 the most they can do is raise it once a year, using the official form and with at least two months' notice.
You can challenge a rise you think is above market rent. Landlords also can't run bidding wars: adverts must state a
rent and they can't accept offers above it. Other UK nations have different rules. (General information, not legal
advice.)

### 8. In the UK, what is the point of the Residential Property Tribunal for tenants to challenge rent increases when even if the tribunal sides with the tenant, landlords can just Section 21 the tenant to override the judgement? *(Quora: unanswered)*
https://www.quora.com/unanswered/In-the-UK-what-is-the-point-of-the-Residential-property-Tribunal-for-Tenants-to-challenge-rent-increases-when-even-if-the-tribunal-sides-with-the-tenant-landlords-can-just-Section-21-the-tenant-to-override-the

**Draft answer:** In England that particular loophole has closed. Since 1 May 2026 landlords can't use section 21
"no fault" evictions. They need a legal ground, such as selling, moving in (not in the first 12 months) or rent
arrears, and the right notice, and Shelter says there's always a court hearing. Rent can only go up once a year with
two months' notice on the official form, and you can challenge it at the tribunal. Shelter says landlords had until
31 July 2026 to go to court on an older section 21 notice. If you're worried about one, get advice. (England only;
general information, not legal advice.)

### 9. Can a landlord deduct anything from a tenant's deposit in the UK, and if so, what is the process? *(Quora: unanswered)*
https://www.quora.com/unanswered/Can-a-landlord-deduct-anything-from-a-tenants-deposit-in-the-UK-and-if-so-what-is-the-process

**Draft answer:** They can deduct for things like unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear, but not just
anything. In England, a deposit on an assured shorthold tenancy has to be protected in a government-approved scheme.
At the end, you and the landlord agree how much comes back. If you can't agree, the scheme offers a free dispute
service. The landlord has to justify deductions, so your best protection is evidence: a check-in inventory you've
checked and signed, dated photos when you move in and out, and receipts for cleaning. Shelter has a step-by-step
guide to getting your deposit back. (England; general information, not legal advice.)
*Before posting: check the scheme and timing details against GOV.UK's "tenancy deposit protection" page on the day.*

### 10. Can landlords deduct money from the security deposit for repainting walls after the tenant moves out?
https://www.quora.com/Can-landlords-deduct-money-from-the-security-deposit-for-repainting-walls-after-the-tenant-moves-out

**Draft answer (UK angle; the question doesn't name a country):** In England it depends on whether the walls are
damaged or just show ordinary wear. Scuffs and fading after years of normal living are usually wear and tear. Holes
from screws, paint pulled off by adhesive strips, or a colour you painted without permission are more likely to be
deductible. Take dated photos at check-in and check-out, ask in writing before putting anything on the walls, and if
you used adhesive strips, take them off slowly the way the maker advises. If you disagree with a deduction, your
deposit scheme's free dispute service will look at the evidence. (England; general information, not legal advice.)
