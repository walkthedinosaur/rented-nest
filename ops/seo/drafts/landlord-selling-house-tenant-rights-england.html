The short answer: no, not straight away. Since 1 May 2026 section 21 "no-fault" notices have gone, so a landlord in England who wants you to leave so they can sell has to serve a section 8 notice using the selling ground (ground 1A), give you four months' notice, and can't use it to start court proceedings in the first 12 months of your tenancy. If they don't get you to agree to leave, they then need a court order – and only bailiffs can remove you. And if the house is sold with you still living there, your tenancy simply carries on with the new owner as your landlord.

Not in England? Different rules apply – see private residential tenancies in Scotland, Renting Homes in Wales and private renting in Northern Ireland.

If the house is sold with you still living there

Plenty of rented homes are sold to another landlord with the tenants staying put. If that happens: [VERIFY all on Shelter "If your home is sold to a new landlord"]

  • Your tenancy continues. The new owner takes over as your landlord on the same terms – you don't need to sign a new agreement or move out.
  • Your deposit stays protected. It should be transferred to the new landlord or stay in the same scheme – ask both landlords in writing who now holds it. See what to do if your deposit isn't protected.
  • Ask for the new landlord's name and an address for notices, and keep paying rent to the old landlord until you're told in writing who to pay.

If your landlord wants you out so they can sell

Your landlord can't just tell you to go. Each step below has to happen in order, and you can stay in your home until the last one:

  1. A section 8 notice on the official form, naming the selling ground (1A) and the date it expires – at least four months away. During your first 12 months it can be served, but it can't run out before the 12 months are up.
  2. A court claim. When the notice ends you don't have to leave. Your landlord has to apply to court – within 12 months of serving the notice, or it expires.
  3. A possession order from a judge, giving a date to leave.
  4. Bailiffs. If you're still there after that date, only court bailiffs can evict you. Your landlord changing the locks or removing your things would be illegal eviction.

Selling is a mandatory ground, so if your landlord genuinely intends to sell and the paperwork is right, the court will usually have to grant possession. [VERIFY: GOV.UK/Shelter – landlord can't re-let or market the home to rent for a period (12 months?) after using ground 1A; state only what GOV.UK says.] Check the notice carefully – our section 8 notice guide goes through what makes one valid.

Renter tip Don't move out before you've got somewhere to go. Shelter warns that leaving too early can mean the council treats you as having made yourself homeless. [VERIFY wording on Shelter.] Contact your council's housing team as soon as you get a notice.

Viewings while it's on the market

Shelter says you don't have to agree to viewings unless your tenancy agreement says you should – and even then your landlord or agent should give notice and come at a reasonable time. Refusing every viewing may cause problems with references or your deposit, so most renters find it easier to agree a pattern that suits them, for example two set slots a week. For an inspection your landlord must give at least 24 hours' written notice; see when your landlord can come in for the full rules.

Offered money to leave early?

Some landlords offer a payment or a rent-free month if you leave before the notice runs out, so they can sell the home empty. That's your choice. Before you agree:

  • Get the offer, the amount, when it's paid and your leaving date in writing.
  • Make sure you have a new home lined up first.
  • Agree how your deposit will be returned – use our move-out deposit checklist.
  • Ask an adviser if you're unsure – agreeing to leave can affect help from the council. [VERIFY on Shelter.]

If you decide to go anyway, you can end the tenancy yourself with two months' notice – see how to give notice to your landlord.

FAQ

Can my landlord sell the house with tenants in it?

Yes. The tenancy carries on and the buyer becomes your landlord. [VERIFY]

How much notice does a landlord need to give to sell?

Four months' notice on a section 8 notice using ground 1A, for tenancies covered by the new rules from 1 May 2026.

Can I be evicted in the first year if they sell?

Your landlord can't use the selling ground to start court proceedings in the first 12 months of your tenancy.

Do I have to allow viewings?

Only if your tenancy agreement says so, according to Shelter – and then with notice and at reasonable times.
