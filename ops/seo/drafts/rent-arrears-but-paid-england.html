The short answer: ask your landlord or housing association for a full rent statement from the start of your tenancy, line it up against your bank statements, and send the differences in one dated email asking them to correct the account. Keep paying your rent as normal while it's sorted [VERIFY source wording], keep copies of everything, and get free advice straight away if you receive a notice – since 1 May 2026, rent arrears are one of the main grounds a landlord can use to end a tenancy.

Why a rent account can show arrears when you've paid

Most "arrears" disputes come down to timing or bookkeeping, not missing money. Common reasons:

  • Due date vs payment date. If rent is due on the 1st but your standing order or Universal Credit lands later in the month, the account can show you a month behind on the day it's printed – even though you've never missed a payment.
  • The statement starts part-way through. A statement that begins mid-tenancy can miss rent you paid in advance at the start, making the balance look worse.
  • Payments put against other charges, such as service charges, repairs or a previous tenant's balance.
  • Universal Credit housing costs paid in arrears or to the landlord directly. [VERIFY how CA/GOV.UK word this]
  • A rent rise you didn't agree to being added to the account – see can my landlord put the rent up?

Step by step: statement, bank records, one email

  1. Ask for a full rent statement from the first day of your tenancy to today, showing every charge and every payment with dates. [VERIFY: rent book rules for weekly tenancies – Shelter]
  2. Download your bank statements for the same period and mark every rent payment.
  3. Make a simple table: date due, amount due, date paid, amount paid. Highlight each line where theirs and yours differ.
  4. Send one email with the table attached (template below) and ask them to correct the account and confirm the true balance in writing.
  5. Complain formally if they won't correct it. Housing association and council tenants can go to the Housing Ombudsman after the landlord's complaints process [VERIFY on housingombudsman.org.uk]. Renter tip Keep every message in writing – email rather than phone, or follow a call up with an email saying what was agreed. If it ever reaches court, your table and emails are your evidence.

Copy-and-send email

Dear [landlord/housing officer],

I've received [letter/reminder dated …] saying I owe £[amount] in rent arrears. I don't believe this is correct – I have paid my rent in full each month.

Please send me a full rent statement from the start of my tenancy on [date] to today, showing every charge and payment with dates. I attach my own record of payments from my bank statements; the differences are highlighted.

Please correct the account, confirm the balance in writing, and put any recovery action on hold while we sort this out. I'll keep paying my rent as normal.

Kind regards, [name, address]

If you get a notice or court papers

Don't ignore it, and don't move out. For the mandatory arrears ground (ground 8) GOV.UK says the arrears must be at least 3 months' rent (or 13 weeks' if you pay weekly or fortnightly) both when the notice is given and at the hearing, and arrears caused by Universal Credit not yet paid don't count towards that threshold. FAQ

Should I stop paying until it's sorted?

No – keep paying your normal rent so genuine arrears can't build up while the dispute is open. [VERIFY source]

Can they take it from my deposit?

Only at the end of the tenancy, and you can challenge any deduction through the deposit scheme's free dispute service – see disputing deposit deductions.
