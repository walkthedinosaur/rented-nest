## Facts (GOV.UK "Assured periodic tenancies: a guide for tenants – If you want a pet to live with you", read 21 Oct 2026 – re-check at writing)
- Ask in writing; describe the pet (type, size, space it needs, e.g. an enclosure).
- Landlord must reply in writing within 28 days. If they ask for more information and you give it, they have the rest
  of the 28 days or 7 more days, whichever is later. If you don't supply the information, they don't have to consider it.
- They can't refuse without a fair reason and should explain a refusal. GOV.UK examples of possibly reasonable reasons:
  an allergy in the household, property too small for the pet, a freeholder/superior lease that bans pets, the pet is
  illegal to own. Usually unreasonable: they don't like pets, past tenants' pets, general worry about damage or future
  lets, or the animal is an assistance animal.
- Once permission is given they can't withdraw it or change the tenancy to stop the pet; each extra pet needs a new request.
- Pet damage can come off the deposit; they may claim on your pet insurance if you have it; not the same damage twice.
- Getting a pet without permission may breach the tenancy.
- If no reply in 28 days or the refusal seems unreasonable: complain to the landlord/agent. Check GOV.UK/Shelter for any
  further route (ombudsman not yet live as of Oct 2026 – check status; don't promise a route that doesn't exist yet).
- Do NOT state rules on insurance requirements or extra deposits beyond what GOV.UK says; tenancy deposit cap rules –
  check GOV.UK before mentioning.
