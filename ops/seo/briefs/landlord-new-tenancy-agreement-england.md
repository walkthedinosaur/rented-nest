## Facts to verify on the day
- GOV.UK RRA overview for tenants (read 19 Nov): most existing ASTs automatically became assured periodic tenancies on
  1 May 2026; tenants with a written agreement don't need a new one; landlords had to give existing tenants the
  government Information Sheet before 31 May 2026; rent can only rise once a year via form 4A; tenants can ask to keep a
  pet and the landlord must consider it and give a reason if refusing.
- Shelter "Tenancy agreements" (updated 1 May 2026): a landlord "cannot give you fewer rights than you have in law";
  rights apply "even if your landlord does not put them in your contract"; once signed an agreement is "hard to get out
  of"; councils can fine a landlord up to £7,000 for not giving written information. Also read Shelter's "Agreements from
  before the Renters' Rights Act" page.
- Joint tenant who died / succession: do NOT write rules from memory – only what Shelter/GOV.UK pages say, else signpost.
- Rent-rise clauses: Shelter Legal says an agreed increase after 1 May 2026 isn't valid unless it follows a section 13
  notice (already sourced on our rent-rise guide) – check whether a "5% minimum" clause can bind the tenant before saying so.

## Angle and outline
Answer first: "Usually no. Most tenancies carried on automatically on 1 May 2026, so you don't need a new agreement to
stay – and a contract can't take away rights you have by law (pets requests, rent-rise rules, repairs)."
