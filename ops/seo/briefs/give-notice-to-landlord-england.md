## Facts (GOV.UK, read 28 Oct 2026 – re-check at writing)
"Assured periodic tenancies: a guide for tenants – How to end your tenancy":
- The agreement sets your notice period, but the landlord can ask for no more than 2 months; if it says nothing, give at least 2 months.
- You and the landlord can agree in writing to end early or shorten the notice.
- Notice must be in writing (letter, email or text are given as examples); the landlord can't insist on a method. Keep a copy.
- The tenancy must end on a rent due date or the day before one. You pay rent during the notice period.
- Change your mind after giving notice → you can stay only if the landlord agrees in writing.
- Joint tenants: usually one joint tenant can end the joint tenancy without the others agreeing; shorter notice needs all
  joint tenants to agree; staying on needs everyone's and the landlord's agreement (or a new agreement).
"Renters' Rights Act overview for tenants":
- Most existing ASTs became assured periodic tenancies on 1 May 2026; tenancies "run on a rolling basis, for example,
  weekly or monthly"; agreements can no longer have an end date.
- Tenancies started before 1 May 2026: separate GOV.UK page "Ending a tenancy that started before 1 May 2026" – writer
  must read it and summarise only what it says.
