## Facts gathered (re-verify at writing; quote nothing longer than a short phrase)
Shelter England, "Cleaning, gardening and decorating" (last updated 6 May 2024, read 11 Nov 2026):
- You only need to clean to the same standard as at move-in (inventory/check-in report is the baseline).
- A "professional standard" clause is only fair if it was professionally cleaned at move-in; a clause making you pay for
  a professional clean is unfair – Shelter says such fees are banned (writer: confirm against the Tenant Fees Act 2019
  guidance on GOV.UK before stating it).
- The landlord can't keep money just because it's time to redecorate; no deductions for fair wear and tear.
- Deductions are possible for redecorating/significant changes without consent (e.g. painting walls a different colour).
Shelter England, "Damage and missing items" (last updated 6 May 2024, read 11 Nov 2026):
- Cleanable carpet stain → only the cleaning cost. Uncleanable stain → usually not the full replacement cost unless the
  tenancy was short or the carpet was new; Shelter's example: 5-year-old carpet, £250 replacement → about £125 might be
  reasonable, even if the landlord upgrades.
- Not your responsibility: fair wear and tear, landlord-responsible damp and mould, damage from slow repairs.
- You can dispute the amount even if you accept you caused the damage; schemes offer free dispute resolution.
GOV.UK "Tenancy deposit protection – disputes and returns" (metadata updated 20 Oct 2026, read 11 Nov 2026):
- Landlord must return the deposit within 10 days of you both agreeing the amount.
- During a dispute the deposit stays protected in the scheme until it's sorted.
- The three schemes: Deposit Protection Service, MyDeposits, Tenancy Deposit Scheme.
Writer must check and source separately: each scheme's time limit to raise a dispute (and whether adjudication needs both
sides to agree – custodial vs insured differ); what to do if the deposit was never protected (link to the "deposit not
protected" guide if it's live, otherwise Shelter).
