## Facts (read 28 Oct 2026 – re-check at writing)
GOV.UK "Assured periodic tenancies: a guide for tenants – Rent increases":
- Rent can only go up once a year, and not in the first year of the tenancy.
- Landlord must use form 4A ("Landlord's notice proposing a new rent") – the "section 13 process".
- At least 2 months' notice.
- Notice can be given in person, by post, or by email only if the tenancy agreement allows email.
- Next increase can't take effect until at least a year after the last one took effect (GOV.UK example: new rent from
  1 Feb 2026 → next increase no earlier than 1 Feb 2027).
- You can apply to the First-tier Tribunal if you think the increase is above the open market rent (the rent the
  landlord could expect re-letting on the open market). GOV.UK page gives no deadline – the writer must find the
  deadline and what the tribunal can do (incl. whether it can set a higher rent, and when the new rent starts) on
  GOV.UK "Apply for an open market rent determination" (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-open-market-rent-determination) or Shelter's
  "Ask a tribunal to set the rent" page. Don't state them from memory.
- Form 4 notices served before 1 May 2026 still apply as stated; the rent can still be challenged.
- Rent review clauses: an increase agreed under one before 1 May 2026 that takes effect after 1 May 2026 does not apply.
Shelter "No section 13" (last updated 1 May 2026):
- An increase by text, email, in person or just through the tenancy agreement – without a section 13 notice – doesn't
  have to be paid. Pressure to agree could be harassment. Section 21 can't be used to evict you for challenging.
