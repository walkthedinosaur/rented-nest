## Intent
A renter feeling cold draughts (windows, front door, floorboards) who wants to fix them now, cheaply, without losing
their deposit or breaking tenancy terms. Second visitor: someone whose landlord won't fix bad windows.

## Angle
Answer first: "Yes – most draught-proofing a renter needs is removable and needs no permission; anything screwed,
glued or sealed in place, ask in writing first. And don't block vents." Practical, renter-first, no legal advice.

## Outline
1. Short answer (2–3 sentences).
2. Find the draughts first – back of hand / incense or candle-free method (e.g. a strip of tissue) around windows,
   doors, letterbox, skirting, floorboards, loft hatch, unused chimney. (No open flames advice.)
3. No-permission, no-trace fixes – under-door excluders, door curtains on tension rods, letterbox covers that push in,
   thermal curtains / thermal lining, removable secondary-glazing film kits (note: tape can lift paint – test a spot,
   remove gently with warmth), rugs over gappy boards. Amazon shortcodes here only.
4. Windows: self-adhesive foam strips (cheap, may not last – EST) v brush/wiper strips; on rentals favour strips that
   peel off and test removal on a hidden bit of frame first; sash windows → ask the landlord (EST: brush strips or
   professional fitting). Silicone sealing a fixed window = permanent → landlord job.
5. What needs the landlord's OK – anything screwed, nailed, glued, sealed or filled (floorboard mastic, caulk,
   new letterbox hardware, permanent seals). Ask by email for a dated record. Oxford City Council: tenants can request
   certain basic energy efficiency measures and "consent should not be unreasonably refused" – writer to verify the
   current legal basis (Domestic Private Rented Property Minimum Energy Efficiency regs / tenant's improvement request)
   on GOV.UK before stating anything; otherwise say "ask your landlord and check with Shelter / Citizens Advice".
6. Don't block ventilation – trickle vents, air bricks, extractor fans and boiler/heater vents stay clear; blocking them
   can make condensation and mould worse (link condensation guide; cite a GOV.UK / Shelter / EST ventilation line
   found at writing time – the EST windows/doors page itself has no ventilation warning).
7. When the landlord should fix it – broken seals, rotten frames, gaps around windows that let in rain/wind; how to
   report repairs in writing (link Shelter repairs advice; link condensation guide). EPC: check the rating on the
   GOV.UK EPC register (find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk) – don't state the minimum band/rules without checking.
8. Moving out: remove strips/films slowly, warm adhesive with a hairdryer, clean residue with a mild method; photos.
9. FAQ (3–4 Qs from secondary queries).
10. Sources.
