## Intent
A renter about to hang a picture, mirror or hooks who wants to know (1) am I allowed, (2) will it cost me my deposit,
(3) how to do it safely. Second visitor: someone moving out with strips still on the walls.

## Angle
Honest answer first: "Usually yes, but it isn't risk-free – check your tenancy agreement, ask in writing, and remove
them properly." Don't promise "damage-free". Practical, renter-first, no legal advice.

## Outline
1. Short answer (2–3 sentences) + "Ad" disclosure (automatic).
2. Check your tenancy agreement first – look for clauses on fixing things to walls; ask the landlord/agent by email
   so there's a dated record (link Renting Tips board ethos; no legal advice).
3. Why strips sometimes take paint off – fresh paint, cheap emulsion, wallpaper, damp/cold walls, overloading.
   3M UK FAQ: wait at least 7 days after painting; not for delicate/paper wallpaper; surfaces must be dry.
4. How to put them up so they come off cleanly – clean/dry surface, observe weight limit, wait an hour before hanging
   (3M UK homepage), test a spot behind furniture. Mention the cold-outside-wall/condensation risk → link condensation guide.
5. How to remove them without damaging paint – pull the tab straight down slowly, close to the wall (3M); if the tab
   snaps: warm with a hairdryer, slice the foam with dental floss, then stretch/peel the rest (3M UK FAQ).
6. If paint comes off – don't panic; photograph it; small touch-ups only with the landlord's agreement and a matched
   paint; how deposit deductions and disputes work (link GOV.UK tenancy deposit protection + Shelter deposit advice;
   the free deposit-scheme dispute service). Don't state what is/isn't "fair wear and tear" as fact – letting agents
   (e.g. cpshomes) say adhesive marks are not wear and tear; say "many agents treat this as damage".
7. Alternatives with no adhesive at all – tension rods, over-door hooks, picture ledges on furniture, leaning art,
   freestanding coat stands (link no-drill storage ideas and small living room ideas). Amazon shortcodes here only.
8. FAQ (3–4 Qs from the secondary queries above).
9. Sources.
