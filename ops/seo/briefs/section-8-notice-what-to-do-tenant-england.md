## Facts (GOV.UK "Notices of possession served from 1 May 2026: a guide for tenants…", updated 20 Oct 2026, read 4 Nov 2026)
- Notice should be on **Form 3A** (pre-1 May notices used Form 3 – separate GOV.UK guide).
- Notice period depends on the ground – see GOV.UK's grounds page (writer must read it; don't guess periods).
- Grounds listed include rent arrears, damage, antisocial behaviour, breach of terms, landlord/family moving in, selling,
  mortgage repossession.
- You don't have to leave when the notice ends: "They cannot take back possession without a court order."
- Court: claim papers + defence form – "You have 14 days to return the form"; possible counterclaim (e.g. unprotected
  deposit, discrimination). Hearing outcomes: outright order, suspended order, adjournment, dismissal. Usually "up to 21
  days to appeal". Bailiffs: usually at least 14 days' notice of the eviction date.
- First 12 months: landlord "cannot start the process" on selling / family-moving-in grounds (can still serve notice).
- Landlord must apply to court within 12 months of serving the notice, or it expires.
- Unprotected deposit (not protected within 30 days) generally blocks an eviction order (exceptions e.g. ASB).
- Help: Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service (free, from written notice), Civil Legal Advice 0345 345 4345 (re-check
  number and hours on the day), council homelessness prevention team before agreeing to leave, Shelter, Citizens
  Advice, MoneyHelper for debt.
